# Sequence of Play & Movement

Card: A

## START OF TURN PHASE

**REINFORCEMENT PHASE**—_Both Players_

- Both players – follow steps in holding boxes on map

## FIRST PLAYER TURN

**ADMIN PHASE**—_Both Players_

- Draw assets/events from selection cup
  - See turn record track on map
- Phasing Player **first** then Non-phasing Player
  - Perform engineering functions
  - Play and resolve events
  - Perform staff functions
  - Use Special Action(s)

**OPERATIONAL MOVEMENT PHASE**—_Phasing Player_

- Place units in reserve, move, build field works, or hold position

**STRATEGIC MOVEMENT PHASE**—_Phasing Player_

- Move eligible units marked with a reserve marker

**COMBAT PHASE**—_For each Area as required or desired_

- **Defender Special Action**—Reinforce or retreat
- **Commit Assets**—Attacker then Defender
- **Declare Point Unit**—Defender then Attacker
- **Reveal Units**—Both
- **Artillery Fire**—Attacker then Defender
- **Ground Fire**—Defender then Attacker

**REACTION & BREAKTHROUGH PHASE**—_Both Players_

- **Reaction**—Non-phasing Player
- **Breakthrough Movement**—Phasing Player
  - Units in reserve
  - Exploitation units
  - Other units via Special Action
- **Breakthrough Combat**—Phasing Player
  - Limit of one combat round per area

**SUPPLY PHASE**—_Both Players_

- Verify control of areas
- Check supply status of all units
- Adjust disorder & recovery markers
- Morale check for Phasing Player's unsupplied units
- End of turn marker removal

## SECOND PLAYER TURN

- Second Player becomes Phasing Player / Attacker
- Repeat First Player Turn sequence above

## VICTORY CHECK PHASE

- Mark current VP level
- Check for Sudden Death Victory

**MOVEMENT RESTRICTIONS**

**Sequence**—Move one unit at a time

**Entry—Must STOP** when entering an area with enemy:

- unit, garrison or task force
- roadblock or defended roadblock

**Exit**—When exiting enemy occupied area, must exit **directly into** friendly uncontested area

**Exit**—Cannot exit enemy occupied area AND re-enter another enemy occupied area in the same turn

**Crossing a Bridge into Contested Area**—Only **1 unit** may cross the bridge (whether intact or blown)

If area is **newly** contested, Defender may try to blow the bridge during the **first** bridge-crossing attempt into the area

**Strategic Movement**—Move any number of areas:

- May only enter friendly-controlled areas
- Cannot enter areas with enemy units / assets / roadblocks
- Cannot cross restricted boundary
- Cannot cross blown bridges

**Strategic Movement**—Limits per Phase:

- German: 1 only
- Allied: # of marked reserves

**German Fuel Shortage**—Fuel asset is required for:

- Any moves requiring more than 3 MPs (except Minimum Move)
- Strategic Movement

# Extended Sequence of Play

Card: B

## START OF TURN PHASE

**REINFORCEMENT PHASE**—_Both Players_

- If the reusable Special Action was not used, move one asset directly from Used Box to Available Box (4.1a)
- Move the reusable Special Action from Used Box to Available Box (4.1a)
- German only: Move one regular artillery asset from Used Box to Eliminated Box (16.12)
- Move all remaining assets from Used Box into selection cup (4.1b)

**Arriving Resources**

- Units placed in entry areas (4.1c & 8.27)
- Task Forces placed in friendly uncontested areas (16.22)
- Events placed into selection cup (4.1d)
- Assets placed into selection cup (4.1d)
- _Exception: Scenario instructions may indicate otherwise_

## FIRST PLAYER TURN

**ADMIN PHASE**—_Both Players_

- Both players randomly draw a number of assets/events from selection cup as indicated on the Turn Track (4.2a)
- Phasing Player **first** then Non-phasing Player:
  - Perform engineering functions (4.2 & 4.3)
  - Play and resolve events (4.2 & 17.0)
  - Perform staff functions (4.2 & 8.3)
  - Use Special Actions (8.25 & 8.26)

**OPERATIONAL MOVEMENT PHASE**—_Phasing Player_

**Movement Action**

- Place units in reserve, move, build field works, or hold position (5.21, 5.22, 5.23 & 5.24)

**River Assaults**

- Mark as appropriate (5.34e)

**Overstacking**

- Correct at end of Phase by retreating units (5.1)

**STRATEGIC MOVEMENT PHASE**—_Phasing Player_

**Reserve Move**

- Move units marked with a reserve marker per Strategic Movement rules (5.4 & 14.8)
- Remove reserve marker from units so moved (5.4)

**COMBAT PHASE**—_For Each Area as Required or Desired_

**Special Action Decision**

- Defender may reinforce or retreat with Special Action (6.21)

**Commit Assets**

- Attacker assigns assets to this battle (6.22)
- Defender assigns assets to this battle (6.23)

**Declare Point**

- Both declare point unit (Defender first) (6.24)

**Reveal Units**

- Both reveal units if a newly contested area (6.24)

**Artillery Fire**

- Attacking artillery fires (6.25)
- Defending artillery fires (6.26)

**Ground Fire**

- Ground units and battle assets fire in the following order:
  - Defenders fire (6.27)
  - Attackers fire (unless attack aborted) (6.28)
- Both place remaining assets from this combat in Used Box (6.29)

**REACTION & BREAKTHROUGH PHASE**—_Both Players_

**Reaction**

- Non-phasing Player moves reserves (7.2)

**Breakthrough Movement**

- Phasing Player moves these units in any desired order:
  - Units in reserve (7.3)
  - Exploitation units, but only into adjacent areas (7.3 & 7.31)
  - Other units, by spending a Special Action for each unit, but only into adjacent areas (7.3 & 7.31 & 8.21)

**Breakthrough Combat**

- Combats caused by Breakthrough Movement are resolved as in the regular Combat Phase, with a limit of one combat round per area (7.4)

**SUPPLY PHASE**—_Both Players_

**CONTROL OF AREAS**

- Both players verify control of areas (9.2)

**SUPPLY**

- Both players check the supply status of all units and place or remove unsupplied markers (9.31)

**DISORDER & RECOVERY**

- Both players remove recovering markers from all units and then flip disordered markers to the recovering side (9.35)

**MORALE CHECK**

- Phasing Player makes morale checks for his unsupplied units (9.4)
- Roll twice in a row for Turns 6-9 (14.93)

**MARKER REMOVAL**

- Non-phasing Player removes ALL markers as follows (9.4):
  - All reserve markers from his units
  - All field works markers from contested areas
  - All road blocks and defended road blocks
  - All enemy river assault markers

## SECOND PLAYER TURN

- Second Player becomes Phasing Player / Attacker
- First Player becomes Non-phasing Player / Defender
- Repeat the Phases outlined for the First Player Turn above

## VICTORY CHECK PHASE

- Determine and mark Current Victory Point Level (19.3)
- Check for Sudden Death Victory (19.4)

## GAME TURN ADVANCEMENT

If no Sudden Death Victory, advance turn marker to the next game turn.

## Standard Effects: Disordered / Unsupplied

- **Reduced Effectiveness**—SN modifier when firing and for morale checks; cannot initiate combat
- **Limited Movement Options**—Can only move 1 area, cannot build field works, cannot be placed in reserve

## Additional Effects: Unsupplied

- **Isolation**—Assets, Special Actions, engineering functions and friendly events cannot be played for such units/areas
- **Command Failure**—May not recover from disordered status
- **Disintegration**—Must make morale check during friendly Supply Phase: Failure results in a disordered result, a step loss, or surrender (9.4)

# Fast Action Battle Series – Getting Started

Card: E

## Familiarize Yourself with Game Concepts

1. Open the box and check all the components.
2. Read Key Words and Concepts in the Rule Book.
3. Read the Examples of Play in the Play Book to understand how the play flows.

## Prepare the Units for Play

1. Sort blocks by color and make sure they are clean. If not, clean with damp cloth and make sure they are dry before applying stickers.
2. US forces use the **green** blocks, British forces use the **blue** blocks and German forces use the **gray** blocks. Within each force there are different colors in the unit symbols, which tell you the individual army (also called the higher echelon)—see table below. You should receive one or more extra blocks for each color.
3. For these instructions, we define the blocks as having **4 edges** (narrow) and **2 sides** (large flat for stickers).
4. Your goal is to present to your opponent a force of blocks that is as uniform as possible.

## Sticker Application

1. **Count the blocks and identify the extras.** Count the blocks of each color and compare the count to the number of stickers for that color. This will identify how many extras you have of each color. Then search through the blocks to find the ones that are the most defaced or deformed and remove them as your extras. Remember that if one face is good, then both faces are ok because the sticker will be applied to the bad face. Likewise, one bad edge can easily be used as the bottom edge of a 1-step sticker.
2. **Check for occasional color variation.** The edges alternate darker and lighter based on the grain of the wood when stained. Place stickers with some having a dark edge and other a light edge when at full strength. This will better keep the strength hidden.
3. **Arrange your selected blocks for stickering.** After you have thoroughly checked all your sorted blocks, arrange them in front of you so that the side you will sticker is face up and the edge you have selected that matches the full strength value of your units is facing away from you.
4. **Apply the stickers to the blocks.** Only apply the left half of the sticker sheet. The right half is a duplicate “spare” set in case any of your first set falls off or needs to be reapplied. Start with a few of the units from the same organization until you get comfortable. We recommend starting with the British since there are only four units: Guards, 43rd, 51st and 53rd Brigades—all of the XXX Corps.

## Protect the Counters

Normally in a hex and counter wargame the counters are handled on the edges, but in this game you will be pulling them out of a selection cup, again and again, and finger oil contact is greatly multiplied. Before punching the counters, you should spray them with a clear-coat finish to protect them from finger oils, which will otherwise damage the appearance and readablility of these counters over time. A clear matte finish is preferred to crystal clear, and crystal clear is preferred to glossy.

## Setting Up the Game

1. Place the map between the players, with the German player on the south side and the Allied player on the north side.
2. Select the scenario to play (see Exclusive Rules).
3. Place markers on the map as directed by the scenario instructions.
4. Sort all units, assets and events by Arrival Turn per the scenario instructions (consider sorting by one of our Play Tips on the back of this player aid).
5. If you have not played a game like this before, we recommend you select the Introductory Scenario and follow the recommended steps outlined on the back of this card.
6. If you have selected the Tournament or Campaign Scenarios, place the units into the pre-marked areas on the map along with the indicated markers (such as field works, river assault and reserve). Place assets marked **S** in the German player’s Available Box on the map.
7. Otherwise, place units, assets and events per the scenario instructions.

## Game Piece Notation

1. Armor or infantry unit class (13.3).
2. Strength and quality symbols (see graphics below).
3. Historical unit designation (lower right corner of unit).

## Unit Strength and Step Losses

The number of pips currently on the top edge of each block has two functions:

**Strength** The number of dice thrown when the unit attacks.

**Step Losses** Each hit against a unit removes a pip of Strength. Show this by _rotating_ the block.

- 4 Steps
- 3 Steps
- 2 Steps
- 1 Step

## Troop Quality

The color of the pips along the top edge of the block defines the current Troop Quality (TQ).

The TQ may change as the unit weakens from Step Losses.

- **Veteran** black pips
- **Elite** red pips with a white outline
- **Green** white pips with a black outline

- Many units retain TQ
  - _This Veteran unit remains Veteran as it weakens_
- Some units lose TQ
  - _This Elite unit becomes Veteran after the first step loss_
- Some units toughen
  - _This unit starts with Green TQ but toughens up to Veteran_

## Entry Area and Turn of Entry

1. Units: All units that start the game on the map have their starting location printed in a white box in the upper left corner. All units that are reinforcements have their turn of entry printed in the upper left corner followed by the entry areas at which they arrive.
2. Assets/Events: All assets/events have their turn of entry printed in the upper left corner. If the asset/event starts the game available it has an **S** printed in a white box.
3. Entry areas span A through Q and X through Z.
4. Allied units frequently can enter at a choice of areas. Two entry area letters indicate the range of choices (AC means the unit may enter at either Entry Area A, B or C, Allied player’s choice).

## Playing Your First Game

1. Pick the Introductory Scenario for your first game (see Play Book). It lists the units, assets and events as well as the special rules you need to play.
2. Get out the units, counters and markers you need.
3. Place the Introductory Scenario map sheet between both players.
4. Read the Key Words and Concepts section again.
5. Look over the Examples of Play – they illustrate the play of a full turn – and study the Combat Phase examples.
6. Read through the Special Rules and Victory Conditions used in the Introductory Scenario.
7. **Now start playing!**
8. Use the Extended Sequence of Play (on a Player Aid Card) to guide you step by step through the phases of each turn. Each step in the Extended Sequence of Play references the exact rules paragraph(s) containing the detail you need to perform that step.
9. All tables and modifiers you need to play the game are on a Player Aid Card.
10. If you supply your own dice numbered zero to nine, treat a roll of a zero (0) as a ten (10). Regardless of any modifiers and what you are attempting via die roll, **a roll of ‘1’ will always result in success and a roll of ‘10’ will always result in failure**.
11. **_Enjoy the Game!_**

## Tips for Play

1. **Speed the set up, the play and the clean up.** For each side, sort and place the starting units, assets and events together in a separate bag or container (one German and one Allied). Then sort the remaining units, assets and events for both sides into the same pile by arrival turn (day). Place each of these combined piles into a separate bag or container for each turn (day). You will have a combined bag or container for the second turn (December 17th), one for the third turn (December 18th), and so on.
2. **Speed up combat dice rolling.** If you have two units in a battle with different Troop Quality (for example, one unit is Elite, the other Veteran), use the same color dice as each unit’s pip value. For example, in a battle with a 3-strength Elite unit and a 2-strength Veteran unit, use three red dice and two black dice and roll them together. The modified Success Number for the Elite unit will apply to the red dice results and the modified Success Number for the Veteran unit will apply to the black dice results. Note that if the two units have the same morale, you can mix and match whatever dice colors you prefer, however, you may still combine the dice into a single roll.
