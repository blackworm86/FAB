### Example 9 — Landing

**Overview—** During the Reinforcement Phase, both players place arriving units in their respective beachheads (Allied player only) or eligible port boxes (both players). These units remain in these staging locations until the owning player's Operational Movement Phase. For the Axis player, the few German units that arrive during the game will remain in the Messina port box [P4] throughout the Allied player turn and will only be eligible to move from that location to Messina and then out to the line during the Axis player's turn. This example highlights various types of landing but the sequences are unlikely to happen in a real game.

**A—** Invading US units have been placed during the Reinforcement Phase of Game Turn 1 and are now ready to land during the Operational Movement Phase of the Allied player. The 3rd US Infantry staged in the D1 beachhead advances directly to Bolognetta [56] to contest the Italian-held defensive position. The advance from the beachhead to the directly-connected coastal area consumes no movement points. When a unit directly advances (lands) into an enemy-occupied area, creating a newly contested area, the attack is a Beach Assault (and is marked with the generic River Assault marker).

**B—** During the Operational Movement Phase of Game Turn 2, the US 2nd Armor in Rocca Busambra [55] moves into Bolognetta [56], costing 1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area. This move not only put extreme pressure on the Axis position, it clears the way for an encirclement planned by the Allied player.

**C—** The US 1st Infantry in Prizzi [50] moves into Collesano [48], costing 1 MP for the road connection but the +1 MP penalty for congestion (entry into an occupied area) does not apply since Collesano [48] is only occupied by an Italian Untried unit. The moving US unit must still stop upon entry. The ensuing combat will likely eliminate the as yet Untried Garrison unit, leaving only one path of escape for the defenders in Termini Imerese [49].

**D—** Finally, the US 2nd Armor in the Palermo port box [P3] must validate that it can advance from the port box: (i) the port box is undamaged (ii) the port area of Palermo [49] is US-controlled and (iii) US-occupied. Therefore, the unit may now advance to Palermo [49], again, without consuming any MP. Since this unit arrived from a port box, it may now continue with a full operational move. It moves from Palermo [49] to Rocca Busambra [55], costing 2 MP for the field connection, then to Corleone [54] for 1 MP (road connection), and then to Prizzi [50] for a final 1 MP (road connection). This move shuts the door on the Italian units in Termini Imerese [49].

### Example 10 — Fading Italian Morale

**Overview—** This represents a number of interlocking rules elements that get driven through (i) Allied capture of airfields and (ii) Allied capture of ports and/or areas of battlefield importance. Depending on the level of the impact, Italian units of the Axis force — whether starting as Untried units (the coastal garrisons) or starting/becoming Tried units — are effected in movement, combat, and supply. In addition, these effects include the reduction in available Axis air support assets.

**Italian Untried Units—** The mechanism that immediately affects the Axis player is that of the Italian Untried unit, which impacts all Italian coastal garrisons in all scenarios. These units are set up in each scenario by being placed face down, randomly shuffled and then placed in areas per the scenario instructions. Given that there is only one veteran unit with strength 2, and only four other (green) units with strength 2, the forces themselves are very weak to begin with. Random hidden placement represents the fact that while some of the coastal garrisons performed quite well despite lack of training, equipment and command guidance, it appeared to be a surprise to both sides as to which of these units put up serious resistance. Players will encounter dealing with Untried units especially during the initial stages of the invasion as Allied units land. There are two mechanics — the initial Untried unit morale check and the inability to receive positive SN modifiers — that are used repeatedly.

**A—** After the Allied player selects the area to resolve for combat, just prior to the attacker conducting artillery fire (if artillery assets were committed), any Italian Untried units in the combat are revealed and must undergo a morale check. Regular morale checks in the game use the modifiers in the Morale Table, for troop quality, and whether the unit is disordered, unsupplied or both. The Axis player now conducts the morale check.

- **Coastal Garrison** in Gela [19] has –2 (for green troop quality) but is otherwise good order for a net Success Number (SN) of 5–2=3. This is expressed as “1 try for a 3” — rolls 2 — success!

This first morale check for an Untried unit is very important. The odds are not good for success most of the time, but avoiding becoming disordered immediately at this point increases the chance that the unit can do some damage. After artillery fire, if the unit has still survived, it may conduct ground fire. The key disability for these units is that only the one veteran untried unit can receive positive modifiers during ground fire. The Axis player (as defender) conducts ground fire.

- **Coastal Garrison** in Gela [19] has –1 (for green troop quality vs. veteran) but cannot use the +1 modifier for the targeted unit marked with river assault (since the 1st US infantry unit is conducting a beach assault). Therefore, the unit conducts ground fire with an SN of 5–1=4. This is expressed as “1 try for a 4” — rolls 5 — failure!

**B—** The Allied player (as attacker) conducts ground fire with the 1st Infantry unit. The following SN apply: –1 (conducting beach assault), +1 (veteran unit firing on green point unit) and –1 (targeted point unit is infantry class protected by field works) for an SN of 5–1=4. If the Allied player had used a Naval Support or Ranger asset to remove the field works just before ground fire, the SN would be 5, and “4 tries for a 5” are very good odds for eliminating the Untried unit outright. If it happens to survive combat, it finally becomes a Tried unit (the Untried unit impacts not longer apply—the unit can operate like any other Axis unit). However, Fading Italian Morale includes a number of systemic disabilities that degrade all the Italian forces as the Allied player makes successful progress.

> Figure: Map around Gela [19]: (A) the Italian Coastal Garrison in Gela is revealed — Morale Check, Roll = 2 = Success; (B) the US 1st Infantry, landed by Beach Assault (River Assault marker), is fired on by the garrison — SN = 4, Roll = 5 = Miss.

**Captured Airfields—** For each of the three Sicilian airfields (Catania [26], Niscemi [18] and Marsala [65]) captured by the Allied player, the Allied player gains an Air Support asset (marked with C for Conditional) and the Axis player loses the use of the next Air Support asset drawn during a future Admin Phase.

**C—** The placement of the conditional Air Support asset into the Allied player's selection cup is immediate (upon capture of the airfield), but does not become available to the Allied player (potentially) until the next Admin Phase. The placement of the Axis Air Support asset into the vacated spot only occurs when the Axis player draws the asset during a future Admin Phase (and he may not draw another asset in its place that turn).

**Captured Ports and Key VP Areas—** For any three of the three Sicilian ports (Syracuse [5], Catania [26] and Palermo [57]) or any of the five areas of battlefield importance (Catania [26], Val di Catania [15], Niscemi [18], Caltanissetta [45], and Termini Imerese [49]) captured by the Allied player, the Allied player places a Fading Italian Morale event (marked with C for Conditional) into the selection cup. When these events are drawn during a future Admin Phase, the Allied player can immediately place them on the Fading Italian Morale Track on the map, each one increasing the level.

**D—** Upon capture of a Sicilian port or area of battlefield importance (yielding mutual victory points), a conditional Fading Italian Morale event is placed into the Allied player's selection cup.

**E—** When a conditional Fading Italian Morale event is drawn, it may be played like any other event (directly in the lowest level slot (from level 1 up to level 3) on the Fading Italian Morale Track on the map). For each one played, negative effects that Italian units face during movement, combat and supply increase. Note that both Catania [26] and Niscemi [18] are critical areas: each contains one of the Sicilian airfields **and** it is an area of battlefield importance. When one of these is captured, the Allied player moves **both** a conditional Air Support asset **and** a conditional Fading Italian Morale event into the Selection Cup. If and when Catania [26] is controlled and undamaged, the Allied player can move yet another conditional Fading Italian Morale event into the Selection Cup! Note that there are only three such events. Further triggering conditions will have no effect after the third event has been placed in the Selection Cup.

(D) The Captured Ports and Key VP Areas box: “Add one to Selection Cup for each undamaged port controlled and for each mutual VP area controlled.” (E) The Fading Italian Morale Track (At Start — only Untried units affected; Level 1, Level 2, Level 3): “Conditional Event Played — Place each Allied Fading Italian Morale event drawn from the Selection Cup here, starting with the Level 1 box.”

### Example 11 — Strategic Movement

**Overview—** The Axis player is limited to 1 strategic move per turn. The island, however, is confined and the need for strategic movement may not appear very often. At the beginning of the game, a rapid redeployment of a powerful unit may stave off a calamitous defeat. One of the important locations on the island is Caltanissetta [45], a victory point location. A powerful Axis battle group is deployed just far enough away from this key town that the Axis player will not be able count on getting a unit redeployed from the west all the way into Caltanissetta [45] itself. Three possible movement routes bring the Axis units to all three of the areas bordering the western side of Caltanissetta [45]. Only the strategic movement option will work. Unlike Operational Movement which enables units to move into enemy-controlled areas, Strategic Movement only enables units to move freely within friendly-controlled areas (never crossing a blown bridge nor moving into an area that is enemy-controlled and/or contested). To use this option, the Axis player places one of the 15th PG units in reserve during the Operational Movement Phase. Then, in the Strategic Movement Phase, he moves that reserve unit directly (without MP expenditure) to Caltanissetta [45]. Once moved, the reserve marker is removed to the Reserve Holding Box.

**Possible Operational Moves—** As a way to compare the value of the strategic move versus the operational move, see how no operational move gets the 15th PG units to Caltanissetta [45]:

- Possible Op Move: from Salemi [59] to Alcamo [58] for 1MP, to Palermo [57] for 1MP, to Bolognetta [56] for 1MP, to Termini Imerese [49] for 1 MP, to Collesano [48] for 1 MP, and finally to Castelbuono [46] for 1 MP and no more MP available.

- Possible Op Move: from Salemi [59] to Corleone [54] for 2MP, to Prizzi [50] for 1MP, and finally to Mussomeli [44] for 2 MP, facing a field connection to reach Caltanissetta [45] without enough MP.

- Possible Op Move: from Salemi [59] to Castelvetrano [60] for 1 MP, to Sciacca [53] for 1MP, to Monti Sicani [51] for 1MP, to Cattolica Eraclea [52] for 1MP, to Agrigento [42] for 1MP, and finally to Canicatti [40] for 1 MP, and no more MP available.

### Example 12 — Basic Combat

**Overview—** The Phasing player (the Attacker) determines the order in which combats are resolved in contested areas. Combat is mandatory in a new contested area, but it is optional in a previously contested area — sometimes pinning your opponent is more effective than attacking. When the Phasing player selects a contested area for combat, a single round of combat occurs — defined strictly in 6.2. Avoid the temptation to treat the individual steps in 6.2 as simultaneous; they are not. Note that the hit priorities for the Defender are different than those for the Attacker. Also note that the Hit Priority sequence should be literally followed after **each** of the four firing segments in a combat (if hits were caused): (i) Attacker artillery fire, (ii) Defender artillery fire, (iii) Defender ground fire and (iv) Attacker ground fire.

**Battle Asset Limitations—** Although players will not usually run into these limits, remember that the Attacker can commit no more than eight battle assets to a combat and that the Defender can commit no more than five battle assets to a combat. In addition, no more than one Italian battle asset can ever be committed to a combat (this limit may be easily forgotten).

**A—** The Allied player (Attacker) must assign assets to the battle in Palazzolo Acreide [6] first with a battle asset (50th Infantry), two artillery assets (24th and 98th Artillery) and one Air Support asset. The Axis player (Defender) then follows with a battle asset (177th Bersaglieri) and an artillery asset (12th Artillery). Both must simultaneously designate a point unit. The Axis player has no choice (the Napoli Infantry) and the Allied player arbitrarily picks one of the two 50th Infantry division blocks. The attacker fires artillery first.

- **24th and 98th** have –1 (target’s point is infantry protected by field works) for a net Success Number (SN) of 5–1=4 and fire with two dice (one for each step of artillery). This is expressed as “2 tries for a 5” — rolls 2, 5 — one hit!

This hit is applied immediately. The first hit priority for the Defender is to remove field works if present (this is the case).

**B—** Now the defender fires artillery.

- **12th** has no modifiers (target’s point not armor nor is it infantry protected by field works) for a net SN of 5 and fires one die. This is “1 try for a 5” — rolls 5 — one hit!

The hit is applied immediately. The first hit priority for the attacker is a mandatory step loss. The Allied player expected this turn of events, which is why he deployed the 50th Infantry asset. Since battle assets can take a step loss, he eliminates the asset to satisfy the artillery hit (placing the 50th Infantry asset into the Eliminated Box).

**C—** After artillery fire, the artillery assets for both sides are placed in the Used Box. The defender can now conduct ground fire. The firing order is not important.

- **Napoli** has +0 (veteran firing on veteran point unit) for a net SN of 5 and fires with three dice. This is “3 tries for a 5” — rolls 1, 5, 8 — two hits!

- **177th Bers** has –1 (green firing on veteran point unit) for a net SN of 5–1=4 and fires with one die. This is “1 try for a 4” — rolls 5 — no hit!

The Allied player has two hits to resolve and decides to press the attack — perhaps another lucky roll by the Axis player for a third hit would have been enough to cause the Allied player to abort the attack. The Air Support asset is not a battle asset, so it may not be eliminated to absorb one of the hits. The first hit is a mandatory step loss, so one of the 50th Infantry units absorbs the first hit. The Allied player decides not to abort the attack (which would end the combat, disorder both 50th Infantry units, and avoid the second step loss). Therefore the second hit must be applied. Since no unit can take a second hit before all units in the combat take a first hit, the other 50th Infantry unit must take the second hit.

> Figure: Palazzolo Acreide [6], first view: (A) the 24th and 98th artillery assets fire (SN = 4; Roll = 5 = Miss, Roll = 2 = Hit) and the field works are Removed; (B) the Axis 12th artillery fires (SN = 5, Roll = 5 = Hit) and the British 50th Infantry battle asset is Eliminated. Second view: (C) the Napoli Infantry fires (SN = 5; Roll = 1 = Hit, Roll = 8 = Miss, Roll = 5 = Hit) and the 177th Bers fires (SN = 4, Roll = 5 = Miss); the two 50th Infantry units take the 1st Hit and the 2nd Hit.

**D—** Now the Allied player can conduct ground fire. Since the entire division is present (both units have the same color for the divisional stripe), Divisional Integrity applies: one extra die for the unit with the lower SN at time of ground fire. He considers himself lucky that he committed Air Support to the combat, considering how much damage the Axis player has already caused. The Ground Support mission can be used in two ways: (i) as an artillery asset or (ii) as a special battle asset, contributing +2 to the SN of one unit. The Allied player has saved the Air Support asset for the latter use.

- **Unsupported 50th** has +0 (veteran firing on veteran point unit) and –2 (versus the Defender’s Terrain Difficulty Number or TDN) for a net SN of 5–2=3. Divisional Integrity applies to this unit of the division (one extra die added to the three dice for the three steps of strength). This is “4 tries for a 3” — rolls 2, 7, 8, 10 — one hit!

- **Supported 50th** has +0 (veteran firing on veteran point unit), +2 (ground support mission) and –2 (versus TDN of 2) for a net SN of 5. This is “3 tries for a 5” — rolls 4, 5, 7 — two hits!

Without the addition of the Air Support, the Allied attack would have been a dismal failure.

**E—** The Axis player has three hits to resolve. The first two hit priorities no longer apply (no field works present and hits were not caused by artillery fire). First, he retreats the Napoli Infantry unit to Vizzini [11] resulting in the unit becoming disordered — this absorbs the first hit. There are two remaining hits and they must be applied. He decides to eliminate the 177th Bersaglieri asset (second hit – placing it into the Eliminated Box) and then must finally take a step loss on the Napoli Infantry unit (third hit).

> Figure: (D) The two 50th Infantry units fire on the Napoli Infantry in Palazzolo Acreide: SN = 3 (Roll = 2 = Hit, Roll = 7 = Miss, Roll = 8 = Miss, Roll = 10 = Miss) and SN = 5 with Air Support (Roll = 4 = Hit, Roll = 5 = Hit, Roll = 7 = Miss). (E) The Napoli Infantry retreats disordered to Vizzini (1st Hit), the 177th Bers asset is Eliminated (2nd Hit) and Napoli takes a step loss (3rd Hit).

### Example 13 — Airborne Assault

**Overview—** This volume provides three types of assaults compared to the previous volume. The standard type of assault, the river assault, remains unmodified. Two new types of assault, the airborne assault and the beach assault are included with particular rules of use. Note that when any one of the assault conditions applies, the attack is marked with the generic River Assault marker and the River Assault penalty for ground fire applies as well. If more than one type of assault can occur in an area, see 14.27 to determine which type of assault (which combat rules and other conditions) will be used to resolve the combat.

**A—** Four units — the two US Airborne units and the two British Airbourne units — use the airborne assault rules upon arrival. During the Reinforcement Phase they are placed in the Airborne Assault Box instead of into a beachhead or port box. Then, at the very beginning of combat, before any other combats are conducted, the Allied player conducts an air drop. The air drop rule requires that the airborne unit only be placed in an area that is adjacent to at least two other areas already containing units from the same army. The targeted areas do not have to be empty. They can contain a combination of friendly and/or enemy units. The following areas in the example are legal for the air drop of the 1st British Airbourne unit:

- Palazzolo Acreide [6] – adjacent to British units in Augusta [13], Syracuse [5] and Avola [4]

- Ragusa [3] – adjacent to British units in Avola [4], Pachino [1] and Modica [2]

- Pachino [1] – adjacent to British units in Avola [4] and Modica [2] and allowed to arrive in this area because it does not cause overstacking

The following areas in the example are not legal for the air drop:

- Overstacking – an air drop in Augusta [13], Syracuse [5], Avola [4] and Modica [2] would cause overstacking

- Only One Area Adjacent – an air drop in Sortino [12] or Lentini [14] is not allowed

- Units from a Different Army – although Comiso [7] is adjacent to two areas with Allied units, the units in Santa Croce Camerina [8] are US units (not in the same army)

If an air drop occurs in an area with an enemy unit, the combat in that area must be immediately resolved before combat in any other area takes place. The drop is dangerous—no hits can be avoided.

### Example 14 — Beach Assault

**Overview—** The invasion primarily involves beach assaults during Game Turn 1. What follows is typical for the combats once landings are complete. The Axis player should expect that coastal garrisons placed forward into the coastal areas are likely to be mostly eliminated. Judicious placement of some garrisons in depth and possibly ignoring some areas on the coast may proof useful for a stronger defense (at the cost of allowing the Allied player an easier landing). The Allied player should expect to get the most out of his Naval Support since its best value is on the coast. Another valuable invasion capability comes in the form of Rangers and Commandos — useful but precious as well. Once landing (in the Operational Movement Phase) is complete, the players conduct the Combat Phase. Except for any airborne assaults (performed first), the Phasing player chooses the order in which the other combats and conducted. During Game Turn 1, all such combats are beach assaults (where the Axis player has chosen to defend on the coast in front of the selected beachheads).

**Note on Combat Order—** In the process of resolving a combat, one area is selected by the Attacker, then assets are placed (Attacker first), point units are selected (Defender first) and finally units are revealed. The two combats depicted in this example are completely resolved one at a time (not in parallel as narrated below)—it is only a convenience that this example treats them simultaneously.

**A—** In Licata [20], after assets have been allocated, point units selected and units revealed, the previously unrevealed Italian Untried unit is revealed (it was placed face down in Licata [20] during set up, drawn from a randomly shuffled pile of similarly unrevealed Italian coastal garrison units). The unit turns out to the one of the typical low-strength green units. Before any fire resolution occurs, the unit must face a morale check—if it succeeds, it will remain in good order, if it fails, it will become disordered before the first shot is fired!

- **Coastal Garrison** in Licata [20] has a –2 (green unit) for a net Success Number (SN) of 5–2=3 and conducts a morale check with one die (for the unit). This is expressed as “1 try for a 3” — rolls 5 — failure!

The unit is marked with a disordered marker (note that its field works remains unaffected).

**B—** In Gela [19] (following the narrative through the other combat), the previously unrevealed Untried Italian unit is revealed and turns out to be the best coastal garrison unit in the game (a veteran unit with strength 2). Before any fire resolution occurs, the unit must face a morale check.

- **Coastal Garrison** in Gela [19] has a +0 (veteran unit) for a net Success Number (SN) of 5 and conducts a morale check with one die (for the unit). This is expressed as “1 try for a 5” — rolls 5 — success!

The Axis player is pleased because this unit has a chance to hurt the Allied units it faces.

> Figure: The Licata [20]–Gela [19] coast with two US beachheads and Beach Assault (River Assault) markers: (A) the Coastal Garrison in Licata — Morale Check, SN = 3, Roll = 5 = Failure — facing the 3rd Infantry and the 1st Ranger asset; (B) the Coastal Garrison in Gela — Morale Check, SN = 5, Roll = 5 = Success — facing the 1st Infantry; (C) the Naval Support asset whose fire Removes the Gela field works.

**C—** In both combat locations, it is now time for artillery fire. Neither player committed artillery assets, except that the Naval Support asset can function as artillery if more than one of them was committed. In Gela [19], the one Naval Support asset by itself is used to immediately remove the field works in the area. Consider this as a sustained pre-invasion bombardment. If the Allied player had committed two Naval Support assets to this one combat, the first asset would remove the field works, and then the second asset would operate as an artillery asset. Now past the artillery fire steps in the combat procedure, it is time for ground fire. We’ll examine ground fire by the Defender in both locations first, followed by ground fire by the Attacker in both locations last.

**D—** In Licata [20], the Defender conducts ground fire. Since the unit is still a green Untried Italian unit, it gains no positive SN modifiers—it must survive combat to become a Tried unit to gain the benefit of any such SN modifiers.

- **Coastal Garrison** in Licata [20] has a –2 (green unit) and a –1 (disordered) and does not gain the river assault bonus (+1 for targeting unit(s) marked with a river assault) for a net Success Number (SN) of 5–3=2 and fires with one die. This is expressed as “1 try for a 2” — rolls 9 — miss!

**E—** In Gela [19], the Defender conducts ground fire. This veteran unit is able to gain the benefit of positive SN modifiers.

- **Coastal Garrison** in Gela [19] has a +0 (veteran unit) and it gains the river assault bonus (+1 for targeting unit(s) marked with a river assault) for a net Success Number (SN) of 6 and fires with two dice. This is expressed as “2 tries for a 6” — rolls 1, 5 — success!

The string of luck continues with the strong coastal garrison facing the invasion at Gela. The Allied player has two hits to resolve on the 1st Infantry units. He might (when faced with such luck) choose to abort the attack, but because this is a beach assault, aborting the attack will immediately place the units into the At Sea holding box (using Sea Retreat). Worse than that, by retreating from the coastal area, the coastal area and the (now empty) beachhead become Axis-controlled. This permanently destroys that beachhead! In this disastrous case, the beachhead marker is placed on the Eliminated Large Units track, counting as victory points (VP) for the Axis player at game end. It is therefore out of the question for the Allied player to abort the attack. Both hits must be absorbed, one per unit.

**F—** In Licata [20], the Attacker conducts ground fire. The Allied force will benefit in many ways: (i) Divisional Integrity (plus one die), (ii) Elite Ranger asset, (iii) Disordered, Untried Italian unit (not able to take advantage of any positive SN).

- **First 3rd Infantry** has +1 (veteran firing on a green point unit) and +0 for the Defender’s TDN for a net SN of 5+1=6. Divisional Integrity applies to this unit of the division (one extra die added to the four dice for the four steps of strength). This is “5 tries for a 6” — rolls 2, 4, 5, 9, 10 — three hits!

- **Second 3rd Infantry** has +1 (veteran firing on a green point unit) and +0 for the Defender’s TDN for a net SN of 5+1=6. This is “4 tries for a 6” — rolls 1, 6, 8, 9 — two hits!

- **1st Ranger** has +2 (elite firing on a green point unit) and +0 for the Defender’s TDN for a net SN of 5+2=7. This is “1 try for a 7” — rolls 5 — one hit!

The Untried Italian coastal garrison unit uses the field works to absorb the first hit and it can retreat out of Licata [20] into Butera [21] to absorb another hit but the third of the six hits eliminates the unit (it is placed in the Eliminated Box). Since the 3rd Infantry unit is not armor class, it cannot achieve an exploitation of this success (exploitations are not awarded for assaults in any event).

**G—** In Gela [19], the Attacker conducts ground fire. The 1st Infantry force will have a hard time knocking out the strong coastal garrison.

- **First 1st Infantry** has +0 (veteran firing on a veteran point unit) and +0 for the Defender’s TDN for a net SN of 5. Divisional Integrity applies to this unit of the division (one extra die added to the three dice for the three steps of strength). This is “4 tries for a 5” — rolls 2, 4, 6, 9 — two hits!

- **Second 1st Infantry** has +0 (veteran firing on a veteran point unit) and +0 for the Defender’s TDN for a net SN of 5. This is “3 tries for a 5” — rolls 5, 8, 9 — one hit!

The coastal garrison unit has to absorb three hits. The first hit can be absorbed by retreating into Niscemi [18] and becoming disordered, but the second and third hits must be absorbed as step losses and it is eliminated. The 1st Infantry division just barely gets the job done. If the Allied player had not assigned Naval Support to this battle, the field works marker could have helped the unit to survive (not a good thing for the Allied timeline).

### Example 15 — Flanking Invasion

**Overview—** There are three counters in the game, two US 30th RCT (–) counters and one British 3rd CDO counter, that have multiple functions (and the counter markers reflect both an “asset” style of layout and an “event” style of layout). Although they can be used purely as battle assets, they can also be used as special independent attacking assets — as the flanking invasion — which is an event. This allows the Allied player to leapfrog an area along the coast and potentially surround an Axis unit that is stubbornly defending the coast. Since we have covered the Combat Phase in a lot of detail, this example will leave out some of the normally occurring strict narrative to emphasize the key aspects of the flanking invasion.

**A—** During the Admin Phase, the Allied player sees the opportunity to bypass the Italian Aosta Infantry unit in Santo Stefano di Camastra [36] by launching a flanking invasion from Cefalù [47] into Sant’ Agata di Militello [34]. This is considered an assault (so it marked with the River Assault marker and suffers the River Assault penalties in combat). The flanking invasion is immediately resolved (there is no waiting for the Operational Movement Phase or the Combat Phase). Nevertheless the strict procedure of the Combat Phase is followed to resolve it. Note Allied player can only commit Air Support and/or Naval Support assets to the battle.

**B—** When the Air Support asset is committed to the Ground Support mission, it can be used either as an artillery asset or as a battle asset. After assets are assigned (two by the Allied player, none by the Axis player) and point units are declared and revealed, the Allied player conducts artillery fire (again — as if it were the Combat Phase). In this case, the Allied player chooses to use one of his Air Support assets as an artillery asset.

- **Air Support** firing as artillery has –1 (target point unit is infantry class protected by field works) for a net SN of 5–1=4. This is “1 try for a 4” — rolls 2 — Hit!

The first hit priority for the Defender is to absorb a hit by eliminating the field works. The Axis player removes the field works marker.

**C—** Now we skip ahead to ground fire conducted by the Attacker (the Axis player did not assign an artillery asset to this battle and the example assumes that ground fire by the Defender was ineffective). The Allied player uses the second Air Support asset as a battle asset in support of the Flanking Invasion asset.

**Flanking Invasion** has +0 (veteran asset firing on veteran point unit), –2 (Defender’s TDN) and +2 (Air Support as a supporting battle asset) for a net SN of 5. This is “1 try for a 5” — rolls 5 — Hit!.

**D—** The defending Italian Aosta Infantry must absorb yet another hit, and it does so by retreating from Sant’ Agata di Militello [34] into Capo di Orlando [31] and becoming disordered. Since the Flanking Invasion has survived this round of combat, it remains in the area. It cannot initiate an attack, it may only defend. Note that although the Italian Aosta Infantry in Santo Stefano di Camastra [36] cannot retreat into this area, its supply line is not blocked by the presence of the Flanking Invasion.

> Figure: The north coast from Cefalù [47] to Capo di Orlando: (A) the “Flanking Invasion” arrow from Cefalù into Sant’ Agata di Militello [34], marked with a River Assault marker; (B) the Air Support asset firing as artillery (SN = 4, Roll = 2 = Hit) — the field works are Removed; (C) the 30 RCT (–) Flanking Invasion with Air Support (SN = 5, Roll = 2 = Hit); (D) the Aosta Infantry takes the 1st Hit and retreats disordered toward Capo di Orlando.

### Example 16 — Retreat

**Overview—** Retreats have been only briefly covered in the previous examples. At times, invasion congestion will cause units to move back more than one area and even back off the island. There is a hierarchy of choices that is, what to try first, then next, and so on. First a unit moves back to an area with zero to one friendly units; failing that, back to an area with two friendly units and then repeatedly back until reaching an area with zero to one friendly units; failing that, staying inside the unit’s army zone but moving out to sea through a valid beachhead; failing that, moving across a restricted or prohibited army boundary (and retreating per the above hierarchy from the top); failing that, remaining in position and losing steps, even to the point of elimination.

**Retreat Options—** The examples will step through these choices in more detail using the British 1st Airbourne unit (in Palazzolo Acreide [6]) being required to retreat from a combat that is not depicted. In addition, this unit has not yet been released from its original army boundary restrictions (it must operate in Zone B). With the exception of the combat area in question, Axis forces are represented by a single unrevealed block in each area — it does not matter what they are, just that the areas are contested from the Allied player point of view. The forces in Syracuse [5], Avola [4] and Pachino [1] are variable depending on the retreat situation below. For example, if an example states that force U is in Syracuse [5], then assume that force V is not present in that area.

> Figure: South-eastern Sicily with the British beachhead boxes B2 (Bark South & East), B3 (Acid South), B4 (Acid North), B5 (Ladbroke) and the Syracuse port box: the British 1st Airbourne unit in Palazzolo Acreide [6] and its retreat routes (A) into Syracuse [5], (B) west across the Zone A/Zone B boundary into Comiso [7], (C) south through Avola [4] toward Pachino [1] and (D) through Avola [4] out to the B3 beachhead; variable forces U and V (Syracuse [5]), W and X (Avola [4]), Y and Z (Pachino [1]).

**A—** Assume that in Syracuse [5], sample force V is present and that in Avola [4], sample force X is present. The British 1st Airbourne unit (hereafter the “retreating unit”) has only one retreat option available:

- Retreat into an enemy-occupied area is forbidden.

- Retreat directly into a friendly-controlled area with zero or one unit is the most preferred retreat path. This is why the retreating unit cannot directly retreat to Avola [4].

- Although Comiso [7] is an option that an aggressive Allied player might like to use, retreating across a restricted or prohibited army boundary is the very last retreat path allowed (short of holding position and losing steps).

**B—** Now assume that in Syracuse [5], force U is present and that in Avola [4] both forces W and X are present (causing the area to be contested). The retreating unit has only one retreat option available again.

- Retreat into an enemy-occupied area is forbidden.

- As the very last retreat option, the retreating unit is now forced across the restricted boundary (despite not yet being released) into Zone A into Comiso [7] occupied by the US 1st Infantry unit.

- If both units of the US 1st Infantry were in Comiso [7] (given this retreat pathway), then the retreating unit would have to continue to retreat out of Comiso [7] to another available area, in this case Santa Croce Camerina [8].

- Since the retreating unit has now violated a restricted army boundary, the Allied player may now release (flip the marker for it in the 8th Army Boundary Restrictions Box) the unit so that it may continue to operate in Zone A. This has the added benefit that this unit can receive not only any Allied strategic assets and events, but it may also receive the support of the British 1st Airbourne asset (since it is considered released for use in Zone A as well). Note that when non-strategic army assets are released in this manner, they still must be used in support of units in their own army.

**C—** Now assume that in Syracuse [5], both forces U and V present (causing the area to be contested), that in Avola [4] force X is present and that in Pachino [1], force Y is present. The retreating unit now faces a longer retreat path with possibly a number of choices.

- The retreating unit has a retreat option that allows it to remain within its own army zone, namely by retreating from Syracuse [5] into Avola [4].

- However, Avola [4] is currently occupied by two Allied units, so the retreating unit cannot remain in this area.

- The primary retreat priority is to find an area, within its own army zone that has zero or one unit in it. The retreating unit, instead of going out to sea via sea retreat, can continue retreating from Avola [4] back to Pachino [1], where only one Allied unit is located. This is a valid and complete retreat path (and the only one available per the rules).

- If Pachino [1] also was occupied by two Allied units, but Modica [2] was only occupied by one unit, the retreating unit would retreat from Avola [4], to Pachino [1] and then to Modica [2].

- The intent of the retreat rules is — if possible — to (i) move retreating units to the nearest non-overstacked friendly area and (ii) keep them in their authorized army zones, even if this means using sea retreat.

**D—** Finally, assume that in Syracuse [5], both forces U and V present (causing the area to be contested), that in Avola [4] force X is present and that in Pachino [1], force Z is present. This is a situation that could happen early during an invasion where the Allied player has not developed any backfield area for retreating units.

- The retreating unit has a retreat option that allows it to remain within its own army zone, namely by retreating from Syracuse [5] into Avola [4].

- However, the Allied player sees that there is no existing pathway for the retreating unit to reach an area occupied only by zero or one friendly unit, and still remain with the current army zone (because both Pachino [1] and Modica [2] are occupied by two British units each).

- The retreating unit in Avola [4] therefore has no other choice but to use sea retreat from the Acid South beachhead [B3]. This means that the retreating unit is moved out through the beachhead to the At Sea Holding Box. The only way back to land is during the next Allied player Operational Movement Phase, using the landing procedure.

### Example 17 — Reaction & Breakthrough

**Overview—** After all combats have been resolved, the Non-phasing player has the opportunity to move units marked with a reserve marker in reaction to what the Phasing player has accomplished so far. He may not move his units into an uncontested enemy area and cause combat. The opportunity for the Phasing player to move units in the Breakthrough Movement Phase is limited to the following conditions: the player may move units (i) marked with an exploitation marker, (ii) marked with a reserve marker or (iii) by expending a Special Action. If this movement causes an area to be contested, the combat in this area is mandatory. A round of Breakthrough Combat (which is otherwise just like regular Combat) must occur due to Breakthrough Movement and it may occur if the Phasing player plays a Special Action in an already contested area. For the examples below, none of the combat rounds of the Breakthrough Combat Phase will be illustrated.

**A—** After the normal Combat Phase is complete, the Breakthrough Phases are conducted. The non-Phasing player has the opportunity to react (with units marked with a reserve marker) to the potential points of breakthrough prior to the Breakthrough Movement Phase. The Axis player had previously marked the Italian Napoli Infantry in Vizzini [11] with the 6th Army Reserve marker (during an Axis Operational Movement Phase). Given the developing situation, the Axis player decides to reinforce Mazzarrone [10] with the Italian Napoli Infantry in Vizzini [11], trying to prevent the destruction of a large unit (the loss of the single-strength infantry unit will yield an Allied VP). A reacting unit (marked in reserve) may make a full operational move during the Reaction Phase, including entering into a contested area, but may not create a newly contested area.

**B—** Now the Allied player begins the Breakthrough Movement Phase. There are two ways to move during this phase: (i) units marked in reserve may make a full operational move and (ii) armor units marked with an exploitation marker or subject to the play of a Special Action may move one area (as exploitation units). In both cases, the Phasing player may create newly contested areas. Given the Axis choice to reinforce Mazzarrone
[10] during the Reaction Phase, the Allied player decides to move a unit into that area to protect the flank of the breakthrough. He moves the US 1st Infantry marked in reserve in Comiso [7] into Mazzarrone [10], with an expectation of securing the elimination of one of the Axis units or better. The 7th Army reserve marker is removed.

**C—** The Allied player now conducts some congestion management in order to prepare for additional attacks on the Axis line. The US 2nd Armor in Niscemi [18] was marked with an exploitation marker from a successful combat (in the just preceding Combat Phase) in which the Axis unit in the area was eliminated by at least one more step loss than necessary. The Allied player moves the US 2nd Armor from Niscemi [18] into Grammichele [17] to create a newly contested area.

**D—** The Allied player decides to use the Reusable Special Action (placing the block into the Used Box) to advance the US 2nd Armor unit in Scoglitti [9] one area forward into Mazzarrone [10] to pressure a weak part of the Axis position. Note that if this unit had been marked in reserve, it could have made a dramatic operational movement into either Grammichele [17] or Caltagirone [22].

**E—** Finally the Allied player decides to move the US 45th Infantry marked in reserve in Gela [19] forward to Niscemi [18] costing 1 MP (road connection) and then on to Caltagirone [22] costing 2 MP (1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP for entering an occupied area). The 7th Army reserve marker is removed. Note that limits on reserve units apply only to Strategic Movement — both moves with the reserve units were operational moves.

> Figure: The Gela–Niscemi–Caltagirone front with the US beachhead boxes A1 (Joss), A2 (Dime) and A3 (Cent North): (A) the Napoli Infantry reacts from Vizzini to Mazzarrone [10] (3 MP), its 6th Army Reserve marker removed; (B) the US 1st Infantry moves in reserve from Comiso to Mazzarrone (3 MP), its 7th Army Reserve marker removed; (C) the US 2nd Armor with an Exploitation marker moves 1 Area from Niscemi [18]; (D) the US 2nd Armor in Scoglitti [9] moves 1 Area using the Special Action (Avail Box to Used Box); (E) the US 45th Infantry moves in reserve from Gela [19] to Niscemi (1 MP) and on to Caltagirone [22] (3 MP).

### Example 18 — Supply

**Overview—** The Supply Phase affects players during their own player turn, so it is important to take actions that leave your units supplied during your own Supply Phase. The worst effects do not immediately take hold, however, which allows players to consider bolder moves. Note that the units in this example are placed to show aspects of the three supply path tracing sub-cases in the supply rules. Also assume that the Allied supply line traces out of the image to the southern coast road and then to the Allied beachhead in the Joss [A1] landing box (as the valid supply source).

**A—** The US 2nd Armor unit in Aragona [43] traces directly through friendly-controlled areas to the valid supply source (Joss [A1]).

- Uses **9.31a** – from a friendly unit to a valid supply source.

**B—** The US 9th Infantry unit in Mussomeli [44] traces from across the blown bridge to Aragona [43] and onward through friendly-controlled areas to the valid supply source.

- Uses **9.31a** – from a friendly unit to a valid supply source, tracing across a blown bridge when starting adjacent to that blown bridge.

**C—** The US 45th Infantry unit in Caltanissetta [45] traces out of the contested area to Canicatti [40] and onward through friendly-controlled areas to the valid supply source.

- Uses **9.31c** – out of an occupied friendly-controlled area (including enemy units)

- Uses **9.31a** – from a friendly unit to a valid supply source.

**D—** The US 3rd Infantry unit in Castelbuono [46] does have a successful supply path and it needs all three sub-cases of rule 9.31. The key for saving the unit is that Caltanisetta [45], while contested, is under Allied control. Note that a supply path operates more liberally than a movement path — the US 3rd Infantry unit could not move directly from one enemy-occupied area to another, but the supply path can do this (given Allied control at the right place in the path).

- Uses **9.31b** – out of an enemy-controlled area

- Uses **9.31c** – into, through **and** out of an occupied friendly-controlled area (including enemy units)

- Uses **9.31a** – from a friendly unit to a valid supply source.

**E—** The case for the US 1st Infantry in Collesano [48] does not look good. There are multiple reasons why this unit must be marked as unsupplied.

- The path to Castelbuono [46] is blocked because the area is enemy-controlled (so the restriction in 9.31b comes into play – the path cannot be traced into or through the area).

- The path to Mussomeli [44] is valid for entry into the area because (i) 9.31b allows the unit to trace out of Collesano [48] and (ii) 9.31a allows the unit to start adjacent to a blown bridge and (iii) 9.31c allows the unit to trace into, through and out of a friendly-controlled area. But then there are only two supply tracing options and they are both blocked by blown bridges. The restriction of 9.31a — the path may only trace across a blown bridge if the tracing unit starts adjacent to a blown bridge — comes into play. Neither of these blown bridge crossings can be made.

- The path to Prizzi [50] is valid for similar reasons because (i) 9.31b allows the unit to trace out of Collesano [48] and (ii) 9.31c allows the unit to trace into, through and out of a friendly-controlled area. But the supply path fails at this point for similar reasons given the two blown bridges.

**F—** The case for the 82nd Airborne unit in Prizzi [50] also does not look good. While it may trace out of Prizzi [50] (due to 9.31c), the supply path stops in Mussomeli [44] due to the two blown bridges. Thus, it too must be marked as unsupplied. The Allied player decides to protect this unit by playing an available Air Support asset using the Air Supply mission. Although the unit must remain unsupplied, the Allied player does not have to conduct a morale check for an unsupplied unit. Failure of the morale check would make the unit become disordered, making its position more tenuous in combat.
