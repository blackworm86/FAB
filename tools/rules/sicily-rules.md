## 10.0 Designer’s Notes

Wargamers need more games that are easy to get into, fun to play, shorter in duration, and with a good feel for the history, while also being challenging enough to warrant repeated play.

The above describes both my feelings as a player and the objectives that I laid out for the _**FAB**_ series as its designer. I aim my designs towards fun, effect, and proper feel, since these are the qualities I want when I play wargames. Much of the underlying details of war have been abstracted out of _**FAB**_, but they were taken into account in the game’s systems to preserve the proper feel. Decisions on multiple levels, with consequences to each decision, are the things that make a game fun for me. The players will be faced with plenty of these decisions each turn.

My focus was to put you in the role of the field commander, and have you decide where to direct your main effort. You must prioritize and assign the precious few assets and Special Actions to the units and battles that you deem most crucial, though all your units are begging for these limited resources.

A major hurdle for this series was luck mitigation. Because of the low block count, combat effects had to change from the standard block game system of each hit scored reduces a unit. By allowing the attacker to abort an attack if more than one hit is inflicted, and allowing the defender to deflect hits through the loss of field works and retreating, and by allowing both sides the option to take hits by eliminating assigned assets, success was achieved in that the units more gradually disintegrated. It is still possible for the attacker to inflict multiple hits on the defender, but this is mitigated by the number of times he inflicts no damage at all on the defending units because they retreated and/or were protected by the loss of their field works marker.

Field works represent a higher quality of defensive works than the simple foxhole dug by each soldier before he slept at night. They represent defending troops using sandbags, barbed wire, mines, and the natural resources at hand to fortify a defensive position, and were often rapidly constructed by combat engineers attached for such a purpose. These positions were most beneficial for the initial contact with the enemy. After the defensive positions were revealed they were of less value.

One decision that went against the grain of the design was having the assets and events displayed face-up. The necessity for this decision was quite simply speed of play. If the assets were inverted, then limited intelligence would work against both sides, and delays in the game would occur as the owner of the assets and events had to reveal them to himself, trying to find the one he wants to play. You are welcome to play with these inverted, if both players agree, but do not expect the game to play as quickly.

I hope that you will enjoy playing this game as much as I have enjoyed refining its various systems. If you have an idea for another battle that you would like to see given the _**FAB**_ treatment, or if you have rules questions, please write me at:

## 11.0 Introduction

Sicily was the largest amphibious assault of the second world war, landing by sea seven divisions (Normandy by comparison landed only six divisions). It was the Western Allies’ first serious attempt to get and keep a toe-hold on Europe, since being evicted by the Axis in 1940 (France) and 1941 (Greece). Neither side knew what the outcome was to be, but as Italian morale faded with each Allied success, the outcome became clear. Now you are in command and you must strive to better the performance of your historical counterpart.

Good luck and enjoy the game!

## 14.0 General Rules

## 14.1 Boundary Types

There is an additional boundary type in this game, which is the restricted terrain boundary (a mountainous terrain depiction). Restricted terrain occurs as follows: (i) between area 31 (Capo di Orlando) and area 32 (Randazzo), (ii) between area 33 (Adrano) and area 34 (Sant’ Agata di Militello, and (iii) **all** boundaries adjoining area 27 (Mount Etna). It takes a unit **all MP** to enter and armor class units may not enter. EXCEPTION—units belonging to the side that currently controls area 27 may use the roads to enter and exit (avoiding the restricted terrain boundary movement cost), including armor-class units, but they may only enter if area 27 is currently uncontested.

> Figure: the restricted terrain boundary (mountainous terrain depiction) around area 27 on the map.

## 14.2 Appropriate Entry Areas

These are areas that units may arrive in on their turn of entry. Definitions of terms and special rules used for entry are provided below.

## 14.21 Allied Landing Boxes

A landing box is an area with an arrow that points to a specific named and numbered coastal area on the land mass of Sicily. These coastal areas are “invasion-capable” for scenario purposes. The Allied landing boxes are labeled A1 to A4, B1 to B6 and D1 to D13.

## 14.22 Allied Beachheads

The beachhead markers are placed in the landing boxes for the beaches the Allies have chosen to invade, where they remain for the rest of the game unless captured. When the Allied player places a beachhead marker in a landing box, that area becomes a “beachhead”. After the first game turn of any scenario is complete, if the coastal area the beachhead is attached to is ever Axis-controlled and uncontested, the beachhead marker is destroyed and placed on the Eliminated Large Units Track. Then that landing box may no longer be used as a beachhead.

## 14.23 Port Boxes

A port box is an area with an arrow that points to a specific port symbol located in a specific coastal area on the land mass of Sicily, which represents a Sicilian port. There are four port boxes labeled P1 to P4: box P1 (Syracuse), box P2 (Catania), box P3 (Palermo) and box P4 (Messina). Either side may use Engineers to damage or repair the three Allied-accessible ports (P1 through P3, see 14.5). Port boxes may be used as appropriate entry areas if the ports are both undamaged and friendly-controlled for the appropriate army shown in each port box.

## 14.24 Unit Arrival

Arriving units are placed during the Reinforcement Phase (4.1c). Arriving airborne units are placed in the Airborne Assault holding box (regardless of stacking) and will conduct an Airborne Assault during the Combat Phase (14.27a). All other arriving units are placed on appropriate landing boxes and/or undamaged port boxes, not more than two units per box.

**a)** **Advance to Coastal Area**—Arriving units are advanced from the landing box or port box to the directly connected coastal area during the next Movement Phase (14.26). Unless an arriving unit is placed in reserve (per 14.24c), no arriving unit may remain in a landing box or port box for any reason (it must try to advance to the directly connected coastal area at some point during the Operational Movement Phase).

**b)** **Remain at Sea**—Allied units not placed in reserve that are unable to advance to a coastal area (because this would cause overstacking) must be removed from the landing box or port box and placed in the At Sea holding box instead. Units in the At Sea holding box must wait to try to enter at an eligible beachhead or port box again on the next turn (according to the Unit Arrival procedure).

**c)** **Arriving in Reserve**—Allied units may be placed in reserve rather than advancing from an eligible undamaged port box to the connected port area. These units may strat-move within the limits of 14.82, but only if the port area is uncontested at the beginning of the Strategic Movement Phase. Otherwise reserve units must land in the connected port area during the Breakthrough Phase; if still unable to do so they are placed in the At Sea holding box (see above).

## 14.25 Specific Entry Areas

**a)** **Axis Entry Area**—Axis units may only arrive by being placed in the port box of Messina (P4), and only if Messina is friendly-controlled.

**b)** **American Entry Area**—American units may arrive on any of the four US beachheads that remain in play, or in the port box of Palermo (P3), if undamaged and friendly-controlled.

**c)** **British Entry Area**—British units may arrive on any of the five British beachheads that remain in play, or in the port boxes of Catania (P2) and/or Syracuse (P1), if undamaged and friendly-controlled.

## 14.26 Movement on Turn of Entry

Once units are placed in landing and/or port boxes, they must advance to the directly connected coastal areas during each player’s next available Movement Phase (except for reserves (14.24c)).

- _Beachhead_—Allied units that arrive at a beachhead expend their entire movement allowance to advance to the directly connected coastal areas. They may not be placed in reserve. If the area is newly contested as a result of this advancement, the attacking units are conducting a Beach Assault (14.27b). Otherwise, if the area is already contested, the landing units may participate in Combat normally.
- _Allowed Ports_—Units that arrive from an allowed port box are advanced to the connected port area and then may conduct a normal movement phase with no movement penalty.

## 14.27 Allied Assaults

There are three forms of assault that can arise in combat situations: River Assault (5.35e), Allied Airborne Assault and Allied Beach Assault. If one or more of these assault conditions applies in a newly contested area (only), a River Assault marker is placed to indicate that the assault penalty applies in combat. Note that the River Assault penalties on the Ground Fire Combat Table apply to all three forms of assault, but if more than one type of assault occurs in an area, (such as a River Assault and an Allied Airborne Assault), resolve the combat as follows:

| Given this combination of assaults... | Resolve as... |
|---|---|
| Airborne Assault and any others | Airborne Assault |
| Beach Assault and River Assault | Beach Assault |
| Any one type of assault | That type |

**a)** **Allied Airborne Assault**—Arriving Allied airborne units (two units each of the American 82nd Airborne and the British 1st Airbourne) are placed in the Airborne Assault holding box (not an eligible beachhead) during the appropriate Reinforcement Phase (4.1c). The two Airborne assets do not use Airborne Assault (they function as battle assets).

- _Air Drop_—At the start of the Combat Phase (before any combats have taken place), all units in the Airborne Assault holding box must be placed into land areas as follows: each one **can only** be placed in an area that is adjacent to at least two other areas already containing units from the airborne unit’s army. If this is not possible without causing overstacking, the unit is eliminated. Note that the selected area may contain friendly and/or enemy units.
- _Assault_—After placement, **all** enemy-occupied areas with a just placed airborne unit in them resolve combat before combat in any other area takes place. The airborne unit must be the point unit. In addition, treat the combat as a River Assault (see Ground Fire Combat Table) regardless of whether other friendly units are involved (and how they moved into the area). Airborne units surviving this combat phase operate normally. An airborne assault **may not** be aborted; every hit inflicted by the defenders must be taken.

**b)** **Allied Beach Assault**—This affects Allied (non-airborne) units that arrive in a newly-contested coastal area via a landing box or flanking invasion (17.22).

- _Landing_—Arriving Allied units are advanced from a beachhead to a directly connected coastal area during the turn of entry (14.24 & 14.26). If the area is newly contested, place a River Assault marker in the newly-contested area. Note that all units in the beachhead box (one or two) are advanced and will participate in the following combat.
- _Assault_—Combat is mandatory. Treat the combat as a River Assault (see Ground Fire Combat Table), however, if the attack is aborted, the attackers must use Sea Retreat (14.28). Note that in this case the beachhead is captured by the Axis and is worth a VP to the Axis player.

## 14.28 Allied Sea Retreat

During the Combat Phase, Allied units in a coastal area connected to a beachhead (only) may retreat to sea if there is no other retreat option (6.6) available. Such units are placed in the At Sea holding box. Any units in the At Sea holding box must try to enter at an eligible beachhead or port on the next turn (according to the Unit Arrival procedure).

## 14.29 Axis Evacuation

During the Movement Phase, Axis units moving normally, may move to Messina (area 29) and then expend one available MP to evacuate to the Italian mainland—represented by the toe of Italy on the map. Once evacuated, no unit may return to play. An Axis unit may use an eligible strat-move to perform an evacuation, strat-moving normally to Messina and then to the toe of Italy.

## 14.3 Army Boundaries & Zones

## 14.31 Allied Army Boundaries & Zones

The army boundaries on the map indicate the areas that each Allied Higher Echelon (army) is responsible for.

- The areas within each army’s boundaries are collectively called that army’s **zone**.
- For reference purposes, the four zones defined by the boundaries drawn on the map are defined as zones A, B, C, and D. The zones comprising each army’s boundaries are defined in the scenario selected (see 22.0).
- IMPORTANT—Allied assets may only be played to support units of their own nationality (even those outside the normally allowed zone) or to affect areas in their army’s allowable zones (see 22.0).
- Units of the US 7th Army and of the British 8th Army can be released via Special Action (14.61) or by event (17.23) from their original zones. The status for each unit is maintained in the US and British Restricted Formation Status boxes on the map.
- Axis units are not affected by Allied army boundaries.

## 14.32 Axis Army Boundaries

None.

## 14.4 Movement Restrictions

## 14.41 Allied Movement Restrictions

Crossing a restricted army boundary is not allowed unless the formation in question has been released from boundary restrictions (14.61 & 17.23). Even then, crossing certain boundaries (and operating in those zones) is prohibited: American units may never move into zone B, except for the area of Messina, which is in both zones B and C. Likewise, British units may never enter zone D.

## 14.42 Movement Into an Area with Untried Units

Either side entering an area that is solely occupied by Italian Untried unit(s) does not incur the +1 MP penalty (5.32) for congestion. Allied units must stop when entering.

## 14.43 Allied Retreat Restrictions

An Allied unit cannot retreat across a restricted army boundary or into an area in a prohibited zone unless it is the only path of retreat available per 6.6. If a valid retreat path exists in the unit’s current army operating zone, including the use of sea retreat (14.28), it must be used before any retreat path crossing a restricted or prohibited boundary can be used. In the restricted army boundary case, this unit’s division (only) is considered released (14.61) without the expenditure of a Special Action. If the unit is prohibited from operating in the area, it must leave the prohibited area at the first opportunity and it may not enter an enemy-occupied or enemy-controlled area as it attempts to do so.

## 14.5 Engineering Functions & Considerations

## 14.51 Additional Engineering Functions (4.3)

An engineer may be used either to damage (Axis) or repair (Allies) one port. EXCEPTION: Messina may not be damaged. Ports may receive up to two levels of damage (damage markers specific to each port show either **1** or **2**). A port may only be damaged or repaired one level per Admin Phase. The port must be friendly-controlled and in supply to perform this function, and for the Allies, occupied by a British (Catania or Syracuse) or American (Palermo) unit.

## 14.52 Allied Engineering Considerations

In cases where a river boundary coincides with an army boundary, the bridging functions may be done by an engineer from either Higher Echelon from its appropriate adjacent area, provided the area in that engineer’s zone meets all the other requirements (4.31 & 4.32).

## 14.53 Axis Engineering Considerations

Axis engineer assets may be used in any zone, and may construct field works (4.33) for any Axis unit.

## 14.6 Additional Special Action Uses & Staff Functions

## 14.61 Additional Allied Special Action Use: Release a Division

The Allies may expend a special action in the Admin Phase to release any one division from having to adhere to army boundaries. Released divisions should have their matching Restricted Formation counter flipped from its “restricted” side to its “released” side in the Restricted Formation Status boxes. 7th Army units that have been released are still restricted from operating in zone B and 8th Army units are still restricted from operating in zone D. For each British division released, one of the three independent brigades (1st Canadian, 4th Armour or 231st Infantry) may also be released as part of the same special action usage. Using single-use Special Actions in this manner will only release units of the matching Army.

## 14.62 Additional Command Staff Functions

None for this game.

## 14.63 Restriction on the Axis Staff Asset

The Axis staff asset may not replace units on the board. Instead, when it uses its replacement ability (8.31), it may move any one Italian infantry (only) asset from the Eliminated Box immediately to the Available Box.

## 14.64 Exclusion of Special Action Use: Reroute Reinforcement

The Reroute Reinforcement (8.27) special action use is not used in this game.

## 14.7 Reserve Marker Eligibility

For all players, units are only eligible to receive a reserve marker if they are supplied, in good order, and in an uncontested area.

## 14.71 Axis Reserve Marker Eligibility

The Reserve markers of the Italian 6th army may be placed on any eligible Axis units, regardless of nationality and ignoring army boundaries.

> Figure: the Italian “6th Army Reserve” marker.

## 14.72 Allied Reserve Marker Eligibility

Reserve marker placement is restricted by nationality for the Allies. 7th army markers may only be placed on green blocks, and 8th army markers may only be placed on blue blocks, even if these units are outside their army boundary.

> Figure: the British “8th Army Reserve” and US “7th Army Reserve” markers.

## 14.8 Strategic Movement Limits

## 14.81 Axis Strat-move Limits

The Axis may only strat-move one unit per game turn. This unit may originate in any zone and may cross army boundaries.

## 14.82 Allied Strat-move Limits

The Allies may strat-move one American and/or one British unit each turn. Strat-moves may only cross scenario army boundaries when the unit in question has been released from boundary restrictions.

## 14.9 Supply Sources & Special Rules (9.31)

## 14.91 Axis Supply Sources

Axis areas, units, and assets all trace supply to Messina, which must be friendly-controlled. Supply traces may cross army boundaries without penalty.

## 14.92 Allied Supply Sources

Allied areas, units, and assets of both armies trace to a beachhead of their own nationality or any friendly-controlled and undamaged port.

## 15.0 Unit Rules Specific to this Game

## 15.1 Motorized Units

> Figure: the Livorno division unit.

The Livorno division (two units) is motorized. These units have six movement points if they do not start in or enter a contested area, otherwise they move as infantry and are always treated as infantry units in combat.

## 15.2 Divisional Integrity

If two units of the same division are both actively participating in the same combat, they receive an extra die in combat (whether attacking or defending). This extra die is given the least effective success number between the two units at the time the dice are rolled.

## 15.3 Italian Untried Units

> Figure: an Italian Coastal Garrison unit (setup code S*).

When setting up for any of the scenarios in the Playbook, the Italian Coastal / Garrison units (marked with a setup code of S*) are placed face down and randomly mixed together, then placed on the map face-down according to specific instructions in each scenario. These units are untried units and remain face-down and unknown to both players until the first time they are in combat. Note that regular Italian units (not marked with the setup code of S*) are defined as Tried units for the purposes of Fading Italian Morale (17.21).

**a)** _Movement Restrictions_—Only **one** untried unit may do something other than hold position in each Operational Movement Phase: it may move, build field works or be placed into reserve. If it moves, it moves with full movement allowance (3 MP). It may not move into an area containing enemy unit(s) unless reinforcing a battle via Special Action.

**b)** _Combat Restrictions_—Untried units:

- Are not revealed until in battle, after point units have been declared and all assets of both sides have been committed.
- Must make a Morale Check to see if the unit becomes disordered (this effect worsens as Italian morale fades) before any attacking artillery fires.
- Green troop quality untried units may never receive any positive modifiers to their success number for ground fire. The one veteran untried unit, when discovered, is elegible for all applicable modifiers..

**c)** _OOS Morale Checks_—Untried units are instantly eliminated if OOS morale checks are required of them in a Supply Phase.

**d)** _Becoming Tried Units_—If these units survive the round of combat that revealed them, they become Italian tried units and then suffer the less stringent effects for tried units as listed on the Fading Italian Morale Table.

## 16.0 Asset Rules Specific to this Game

## 16.1 Axis Asset General Rules

Axis assets may be played in any zone and support both Axis nationalities.

## 16.11 Italian Battle Assets

While they may support any Axis nationality, no more than one Italian Battle Asset may be committed to any battle (attacking or defending).

## 16.12 Other Axis Assets

All German assets and the Italian artillery assets have no special restrictions.

## 16.2 Allied Asset General Rules

Allied assets that are army specific may only assist units of their own army (7th or 8th). Strategic assets may be assigned to support either army.

## 16.21 Naval Support Assets

> Figure: the Naval Support asset.

The Allies start with four naval support assets. These can be used as normal artillery assets, except that they can only be used in coastal areas.

- _Shock and Awe_—For each combat in a coastal area, one naval support asset can be used to remove the defending field works, instead of firing as artillery. This happens as the first act of each battle, before the second naval support asset fires as artillery, if two were committed.
- _Reduction of Support_—During the Reinforcement Phase, if there is at least one naval support asset in the Used Box, move one to the Eliminated Box. This represents the fleet’s gradual withdrawal from harm’s way.

## 16.22 US Rangers

> Figure: the 1st Ranger asset.

Ranger assets (each marked with the special Ranger asset depiction) may, at the Allied player’s option, automatically remove the enemy’s Field Works marker instead of rolling their combat die. This removal is done before calculating the success number for other units and assets involved in the same battle. The attack must not have been aborted and the Ranger must have survived defensive fire to perform this option.

## 16.23 British Commandos

The one British Commando asset (the 3rd CDO—marked with the special Ranger asset depiction) has the same combat option listed for the American Rangers in 16.22 above. Additionally, once per game only, this asset may be used as an event to make a flanking invasion (see 17.22) during any Admin phase.

## 16.3 Air Support Assets (Both Sides)

> Figure: the Air Support asset.

Air support assets are flexible and can be used in the following ways regardless of the supply status of the area to which they are committed.

## 16.31 Dogfighting

Use during the Admin Phase (cancel an Interdiction Mission), the Combat Phase (cancel a Ground Support Mission) or the Supply Phase (cancel an Air Supply Mission).

- _How to Use_—If either player commits an air support asset to any of the missions below, the other player may cancel the stated mission by playing one of his own air support assets, in addition to the allowed artillery and battle assets.
- _Results_—Both assets are placed in the Used Box and neither player may commit more assets to replace the cancelled mission.

## 16.32 Ground Support Mission

Use during the Combat Phase.

- _How to Use_—Play during the Assign Assets steps of the Combat Round (6.22 & 6.23). Only one air support asset per side may be committed to each battle.
- _Dogfighting Sequence_—The first player to commit an air support asset to a battle (attacker commits assets first) allows the other player to play an air support asset for Dogfighting, if desired, as his allowed air commitment to the battle. If the defender is the first to commit an air support asset to the battle (6.23), the attacker may play an air support asset for Dogfighting – out of sequence (an exception to the sequence in the Combat Round) – as his allowed air commitment to the battle.
- _Results_—If a Ground Support mission (played by either or both players) is not cancelled by Dogfighting, an air support asset committed to a battle is used in one of the following two ways. Afterwards, it is placed in the Used Box, even if the attack was aborted.
- As an artillery asset, it fires when friendly artillery fire is resolved, but it does not count against the limit of useable artillery assets for the combat. The choice to use the Ground Support mission as an artillery asset must be made before friendly artillery fire is rolled.
- As a battle support asset, it gives direct support to one unit present, owning player’s choice, providing a **+2** modifier to that unit’s Success Number. A step-loss may **not** be assigned to the Ground Support mission.

## 16.33 Interdiction Mission

Use during the Admin Phase.

- _How to Use_—Place the air support asset on a land area during the Admin Phase.
- _Results_—The opposing player must pay one additional movement point to enter **and** to leave the affected area. The opposing player may **not** strat-move into or out of the interdicted area. The asset remains in the affected area until the Supply Phase of the same Player-turn, when it is moved to the Used Box.

## 16.34 Air Supply Mission

Use during the Supply Phase.

- _How to Use_—Place the air support asset on one unsupplied area at any time during the Supply Phase.
- _Results_—The area, the friendly units, and any assets with them are now supplied as follows: (1) the unsupplied marker is not removed and the effects of 9.34a (no assets assigned) and 9.34d (no events, Special Actions, Staff Functions or engineering functions allowed) are still in force **but** (2) the effects of 9.34b (morale check) and 9.34c (no disorder adjustment) are **not** in effect during this Supply Phase. The asset is then placed in the Used Box.

## 16.4 Airfields and Their Effect on Air Support Assets

The Allies have three conditional air support assets (marked with a **C** as their turn of arrival). These are placed, one each, in the three holding boxes of the Captured Airfields Track.

Each time that the Allies capture one of the three Sicilian airfields for the first time, one of these Allied conditional assets is immediately placed in the Selection Cup. Additionally, the Axis Player must fill the now empty holding box with the next Axis air support asset that arrives into the Available Box from the Selection Cup (no effect on air support assets already in the Available or Used Boxes). Players move eliminated units, assets and events to their respective Eliminated Boxes and place any large eliminated units on the Large Eliminated Units Track (19.2).

## 16.5 Ports and Their Effect on Allied Asset Draws

In addition to their effect on Fading Italian Morale (see 17.21), capturing intact ports has a positive effect on Allied asset/event draws. For each undamaged Sicilian port the Allies both control and currently occupy with at least one unit, add one to the asset/event chits that the Allies are allowed to draw during each Admin Phase. _For Example, if it is turn 7 and the Allies control two undamaged ports, they would draw 13/7 instead of 11/5 for that turn._

## 17.0 Event Descriptions

## 17.1 Axis Events

Axis events are not restricted by zone or nationality.

## 17.11 Patton Soldier Slap

> Figure: the “Patton Soldier Slap” event (turn 8, strategic).

_Patton’s staff had to divert some of their energies into damage control after Patton slapped two soldiers in field hospitals. Historically this happened in early August, but it could have occurred earlier._

- This event is played during an Admin Phase, and stays in effect through the remainder of the game.
- The effect of this event is that it will now take the expenditure of **two** single-use US Special Action events to allow their use either to perform a Special Action or for the alternate event use (4.21). This restriction does not apply to the reusable Special Action or British single-use Special Actions.

## 17.12 Axis Tactical Extract

> Figure: the “Axis Tactical Extract” event (turn 5, strategic).

When played, all German (not Italian) units are allowed to move directly from a contested area into an adjacent enemy-controlled and/or another adjacent contested area for the entire ensuing movement phase. The areas moved between may not be newly contested, nor may the area moved to be further away from Messina than the area exited (in terms of areas distant). The moving German units are marked as Recovering at the end of this move (they cannot attack, but will be in good order for the Allied player turn).

## 17.2 Allied Events

Unless an Allied event indicates that it is specific to one army, it may be used without regard to nationality or army boundary.

## 17.21 Fading Italian Morale

> Figure: the “Fading Italian Morale” event (conditional, C).

These events have a C instead of a turn number for their turn of arrival, indicating that they are conditional. There are three of them and they are initially placed in the Captured Ports and Key VP Areas box on the Allied side of the map. Each time a triggering action occurs, one Fading Italian Morale event is added to the Allied selection cup until all three have been added.

**a)** _Triggering Actions_—The triggering actions are:

- _Functional Port Captured_—Add an available event to the Allied selection cup the instant that each Sicilian port is both Allied-controlled and undamaged for the first time.
- _Area of Battlefield Importance Captured_—Add an available event to the Allied selection cup the instant the Allies first capture each of the five areas of battlefield importance (yellow stars) for the first time.
- _Catania (Area 26)_—This area can trigger both of the above actions either simultaneously or independently.

**b)** _Level of Italian Morale_—There are three levels of worsening Italian Morale.

- _Level 1, 2 & 3_—When the first, second and third (last) Fading Italian Morale event is drawn from the Allied selection cup and is played for its listed event, each one is placed on the Fading Italian Morale Track on the Axis side of the map in the corresponding Level 1, 2 or 3 box.

For each level of Italian Morale, there are negative impacts defined below. All of these defined effects are briefly summarized on a player aid.

**c)** _Impact on Axis Asset/Event Draws_—These effects occur during the Admin Phase when the Allied Player plays a Fading Italian Morale event for its listed event.

- _Level 1_—When the **first** event is played, the Axis Player immediately moves **one** Italian asset in the Available Box to the Eliminated Box.
- _Level 2_—When the **second** event is played, first the Axis Player and then the Allied Player immediately **each** select one Italian asset in the Available Box and moves it to the Eliminated Box. Up to a total of two Italian assets could be eliminated.
- _Level 3_—When the **third** event is played, first the Axis Player and then the Allied Player immediately **each** select **two** Italian assets in the Available Box and moves them to the Eliminated Box. Up to a total of four Italian assets could be eliminated.
- In all of the above cases, The Staff Asset may not be selected to be eliminated

**d)** _Impact on Italian Untried Units_—These effects occur during the Reveal Units step (6.24) of the Combat Phase or when an OOS morale check is required. The level in effect is based on how many Fading Italian Morale events have been drawn in prior Admin Phases and placed onto the Faded Italian Morale Track.

- _At Start_—Only the Italian Untried Unit morale check is in effect (15.3).
- _Level 1_—All Italian Untried Units in the combat are automatically disordered.
- _Level 2 & 3_—All Italian Untried Units in the combat are automatically disordered and then each must make a morale check. Any unit that fails this morale check suffers a step loss and may possibly surrender (17.21f).

**e)** _Impact on Italian Tried Units_—These effects occur during the Reveal Units step (6.24) of the Combat Phase whether or not the units are already revealed. The level in effect is based on how many Fading Italian Morale events have been drawn in prior Admin Phases and placed onto the Faded Italian Morale Track.

- _Level 1_—All good order and recovering Italian Tried Units in the combat must make a morale check. Any unit that fails this morale check is disordered.
- _Level 2_—All Italian Tried Units in the combat are automatically disordered.
- _Level 3_—Any already disordered Italian Tried units in the combat each must make a morale check. Any unit that fails this morale check suffers a step loss and may possibly surrender (17.21f). All good order and recovering Italian Tried Units in the combat are automatically disordered.

**f)** _Surrender_—If an Italian unit fails a required morale check in the Reveal Units step (6.24) of Combat, use the morale check roll to determine whether the unit surrenders: veteran disordered units that roll a **10** or green disordered units that roll an **8-10** surrender. The surrendering unit is eliminated. Any attacking armor receives an exploitation opportunity if the area is no longer contested, either from step-loss or surrender.

## 17.22 Flanking Invasion

> Figure: the 30 RCT (-) and 3rd CDO battle assets.

There are three 1-pip battle assets (two US assets, each representing different battalions of the 30th RCT, and one British asset, the 3rd CDO) that have a dual purpose as Flanking Invasion events. When used as an event, one of these assets may be placed during the Admin Phase onto an enemy-controlled, uncontested coastal area within two areas of any unit from the same army that is also in a coastal area (only one per Admin Phase).

- If the area is enemy-occupied, a round of combat is immediately fought during the Admin Phase of the landing with asset constraints (see below). The flanking invasion asset is considered to be making a beach assault (14.27b) for purposes of success number modification.
- The Allied player can only commit air support and/or naval support assets (not artillery or battle assets) during the landing and for as long as the flanking invasion remains in the area. The Axis player has no unusual restrictions.
- If the flanking invasion survives this round of combat, or if the area was unoccupied, the asset will remain in the area as follows until it is eliminated in combat or relieved: (i) it may not initiate combat (defend only), (ii) although it does not control the area, enemy units cannot retreat into the area, (iii) it does not have to trace supply, and (iv) it does not block enemy supply.
- The asset is relieved the instant an Allied unit enters the area by being placed in the Used Box. Note that 3rd CDO asset may only be used once per game as a flanking invasion event while a 30th RCT asset may be used for its flanking invasion capability each time it is in the Available Box during an Admin Phase.

## 17.23 US 7th Army Release

> Figure: the “US 7th Army Release” event (turn 3).

All six US divisions are released from having to adhere to Army boundaries (except that they still cannot enter zone B). This allows them to now enter zone A or zone D, depending on the invasion scenario chosen. Released divisions should have their matching Restricted Formation counter flipped from its “restricted” side to its “released” side in the Restricted Formation Status boxes.

## 17.24 Infantry Replacement

> Figure: the “Br Inf Non-Elite Repl” (turn 2) and “US Inf Non-Elite Repl” (turn 8) events.

This event may be used to provide one **non-elite step** to increase the current strength of any supplied, **reduced** infantry-class unit of the indicated nationality, even in a contested area. Eliminated units may not be returned by use of a replacement event.

## 17.25 Armor Replacement

Used exactly as the infantry replacement above, except that it affects armor-class units.

## 18.0 Optional Rules

_These rules add more “chrome” to the game. Use and mix these rules as desired when agreed upon by both players._

## 18.1 Airborne Units

The American 82nd and the British 1st Airborne Divisions were specially trained and equipped to operate behind enemy lines with minimal supply. The four units of these two divisions (only) ignore the usual **–1** unsupplied morale check modifier (6.7).

## 18.2 Advanced Artillery Modifiers

_Artillery is generally less effective against defenders and more effective against infantry-class targets than armor-class targets._

Add the following Success Number Modifiers for artillery fire:

**–1** Target is the Defender

**+1** Target’s point unit is infantry-class and not in Field Works.

## 18.3 Overrunning Artillery

If the Defender had an artillery asset in a combat and the area was vacated of defending units, there is a chance the defending artillery asset will be overrun and destroyed. If the Defender retreated, but at least one step survived, the artillery asset is destroyed on a die-roll with a Success Number of **1**. If the defending units and battle assets were destroyed, the Success Number becomes **2**. Add one to the Success Number for each unabsorbed hit from the combat. Roll one die for each involved defending artillery asset.

## 18.4 Maintaining Field Works

Instead of removing Field Works from a contested area automatically during a Supply Phase, have the controlling player make a morale check for one unit of his choice within the area during friendly Supply Phases (only). The Field Works marker is only removed by absorbing a hit or by failure of this morale check.

## 19.0 Determining Victory

Players earn Victory Points (VP’s) by securing Objective Areas and by eliminating beachheads and certain enemy units.

## 19.1 Objective Areas Victory Points

An Objective Area must be both friendly-controlled AND be able to trace a valid supply path (9.31) for a player to be awarded its VP(s) during the Victory Check Phase. Objective Area symbols show who is awarded the VP’s for control of them as follows:

- **Areas Near Messina**—These areas have German crosses marking them. Only the Allied player is awarded VP’s for control of these areas and only if currently occupied by at least one Allied unit.
- **Coastal Areas of Importance far from Messina**—These areas have white stars marking them. Only the German player is awarded VP’s for control of these areas.
- **Areas of Battlefield Importance**—These areas have yellow stars marking them. Both players are awarded VP’s for control of these areas. The Allies are only awarded VP’s for these areas if they are currently occupied by at least one Allied unit.

## 19.2 Eliminated Large Unit Adjustment

Each eliminated unit which has three or more steps on the unit label is worth one VP to the opposing player. Such units should be placed on the Eliminated Large Units Track as they are eliminated. Smaller units and assets do not have a VP value. Captured Allied beachhead markers are also worth a point each to the Axis player and are also placed on the Eliminated Large Units Track.

## 19.3 Calculating Victory Points

> Figure: the VPs marker.

During the Victory Check Phase of each turn, both players sum the VP’s they are awarded for Objective Areas they control per 19.1 above, then add the number of eliminated enemy large units (which may include captured Allied beachhead markers), and then compare those totals. The difference between those totals is the current VP level, with the player having the higher total being the advantaged player. Use the VP marker provided, which has a German side and an Allied side, placing it on the General Information Track to reflect the advantaged player and the current VP level.

## 19.4 Sudden Death Victory

Beginning with turn 4, the Turn Record Chart shows what is needed in terms of net Victory Points (yours minus his) to end the game in a Sudden Death Victory during the Victory Check Phase for each of these turns. If neither player has achieved the net Victory Points needed to end the game, play proceeds to the next Game Turn.
