## 20.0 Designer’s Notes

This game took a bit longer than I intended to bring to your table, but I wanted it to be right. I feel confident that **_FAB volume II_** is indeed ready for action and I feel that it even surpasses the gaming experience we enjoyed in volume I.

The main problem was scale, as this campaign involved far fewer divisions that the battle of the bulge, but the amount of territory was similar. The terrain in Sicily is very rugged, and the battle eventually results in a very narrow front as Messina is approached.

I eventually settled on a scale of one battalion per pip (about half the scale of volume I) and made each division two units and an asset, and also added a divisional integrity rule to effectively encourage players to keep their divisions together.

The next challenge was to effectively divide the island into areas. You will notice more areas that meet at a point in Sicily, this is intentional and an aspect of the difficult terrain.

After a very long time fine-tuning everything to my satisfaction, I took the game for a test drive, and the long effort paid off handsomely in a game that was both fun and gave the correct feel of the campaign.

I have had enormous fun playtesting and fine tuning the game you now hold in your hands. It is my sincere hope it brings you the same fun and excitement for many years to come.

I apologize for the delay in getting volume II into your hands, but I hope you’ll agree that it was worth the wait.

Enjoy the game!

—_Rick Young_

## 21.0 Player’s Notes

## 21.1 Axis Player

The Axis player needs to hold on, as long as he can, to the airfields, ports, and battlefield objectives. Always be on the lookout for opportunities where the Allies have left a lone block or a critical area exposed to possible counterattack, but be careful you are not placing your counterattacking units in a noose.

As Italian Morale fades, you will have no choice but to shorten your line and back up towards Messina, with an eye on getting your Italians out of the line before they afford the Allies a few easy VPs.

Special Actions are usually best spent buying replacement steps for your German units. As your Italian non-garrison units get reduced to 1-step, pull them off the line and get them off the island. They are not worth anything to you alive, but they are worth a VP each to the Allies if they can hunt them down.

## 21.2 Allied Player

You have a tough job ahead. You must put pressure all along the line and try to stretch the Axis front to the breaking point.

The Luftwaffe will be quite annoying in cancelling your own airpower until you start securing the airfields.

Make use of your flanking invasions, they will interdict movement and cut retreat paths, forcing the Axis to deal with them rather than strengthening his front.

You have a lot of assets that can negate enemy field works (rangers, commandos, naval support, and engineers), use one whenever possible as enemy field works will otherwise slow you down too much.

## 22.0 Scenarios

## 22.1 Introductory Scenario: Sunrise Bridge

## 22.11 Overview

Use this short scenario to introduce yourself to the rules. The goal is simple — seize and control the special VP areas marked right on the small map.

## 22.12 Scenario Length

Play Game Turns 1-4 only.

## 22.13 Area of Play

- Use the Sunrise Bridge map sheet included in the game. Only the areas on this map sheet are in play. All land areas start Axis-controlled.
- Use the Scenario Forces sheet included in the game. It contains the complete listing of forces (units, assets and events) at start and subsequent turns.
- Use the Scenario Information sheet included in the game. It contains the necessary holding boxes, time track and asset draws for the scenario.
- Place the Scenario Forces sheet and Scenario Information sheet next to the Sunrise Bridge map sheet for both players to use. The main map is not needed to play.

## 22.14 Deployment at Start

Reference the Scenario Forces sheet for the units deployed at start on the map (Axis) or arriving during the Reinforcement Phase (Allied)

- _Axis Deployment of Regular Units_—First the Axis player deploys German and Italian units with a starting area number in those locations at full strength.
- _Axis Deployment of Coastal Garrisons_—Then the Axis player deploys the ten Italian Coastal Garrisons (units with an S*) as follows: all these units are shuffled face-down and placed, one per area, on the Sunrise Bridge map sheet in areas 1-7, 13, 14 and 26.
- _Axis Fortification_—Finally the Axis player places a field works marker on each coastal area that (a) is invasion-capable and (b) has at least one Axis unit in the area.
- _Allied Landing_—The Allied player places the five British beachhead markers in the following landing boxes: B1 through B5. Once the deployment of beachhead markers is done, the Allied player places his units onto the beachheads and the Airborne Assault holding box (14.24), which completes the Reinforcement Phase of Game Turn 1.

## 22.15 Reinforcements

Reference the Scenario Forces sheet for the arriving units, assets and events for both sides. All units arrive at full strength.

## 22.16 Special Scenario Rules

All Series and Exclusive rules are in effect unless modified by this scenario.

- British forces are constrained to land and operate in Zone B (14.3 & 14.4) unless specifically released by use of special action (14.61) to enter into areas in Zone A (areas 7, 10, 11, 17, 16 and 25).
- Neither player may voluntarily exit units off the map, with the exception of Allied Sea Retreat (14.28); the Scenario Information sheet contains the At Sea holding box for Sea Retreat. Otherwise, units forced off the map for any reason are considered eliminated.
- The requirements and restrictions for Italian Untried Units are in effect (15.3) including the avoidance of a movement congestion penalty (14.42).
- Fading Italian Morale (17.21) as triggered by the conditional assets (16.4) and events (16.5) is not used.
- The Axis supply source rule (14.91) is modified: a unit is in supply if it can trace a supply path into one of the following friendly-controlled areas: areas 16, 17, 25 or 26. The Allied supply source rule (14.92) is unchanged.

## 22.17 Victory

Victory is determined at the end of Turn 4 by awarding VP for control of the following areas:

- 1 VP for control of Areas 6, 7 and 10 through 17
- 2 VP for control of Areas 5 and 16
- 3 VP for control of Area 25
- 4 VP for control of Catania (area 26)

These values are included on the Sunrise Bridge map sheet. Elimination of large units does not apply in this scenario. After the VP for area control is calculated for both players, the difference between the two values is calculated. The player with positive net VP is the winner, otherwise the game is a draw.

The British 8th Army faced unexpected difficulty in pushing up the Sicilian coast towards Mount Etna, a naturally impressive and forbidding defensive feature used to great advantage by the Italian 6th Army.

## 22.2 The Tournament Scenario

## 22.21 Overview

This is the standard scenario, representing the period from the landings on July 10, 1943 through July 19, 1943. Playing this game to completion should take experienced players about three hours. The Allied player must press the attack as strongly as possible, including the achievement of one or more breakouts and/or encirclements of the Axis positions.

## 22.22 Scenario Length

Play 5 Game Turns unless a Sudden Death Victory is achieved.

## 22.23 Area of Play

The entire map is used. All land areas of Sicily are Axis-controlled.

## 22.24 Deployment at Start

- _Units, Assets and Events at Start_—All scheduled units will be deployed (Axis) or arrive (Allied) at full strength. All scheduled assets and events are placed in the Available Box but note the Turn 1 Sequence Exception (22.27). See the At Start reference cards for both players.
- _Axis Deployment of Regular Units_—First the Axis player deploys German and Italian units with a starting area number in those locations at full strength.
- _Axis Deployment of Italian Coastal Garrisons_—Then the Axis player deploys Italian Coastal Garrisons (units with an S*) as follows: all these units are shuffled face-down and placed, one per area, anywhere in Sicily that the Axis Player desires, including areas that already contain a tried unit, but not areas that already contain two tried units.
- _Axis Fortification_—Finally the Axis player places a field works marker on each coastal area that (a) is invasion-capable and (b) has at least one Axis unit in the area.
- _Allied Invasion Planning_—Now the Allied player examines the Axis setup and may freely choose one of the following two invasion choices: (a) the **Historical Landing Plan** (22.25) or (b) the **Alternate Landing Plan** (22.26).
- _Allied Landing_—Once the deployment of beachhead markers is done (according to the selected landing plan), the Allied player places his invasion units (with an **Inv**) onto the beachheads and the Airborne Assault holding box (14.24), which completes the setup.

## 22.25 The Historical Landing Plan

- _British Deployment_—The Allied player chooses any five consecutive landing boxes in **zone B** and places the 5 British beachhead markers in them. British units may only operate in zone B unless released from boundary restrictions (allowing them to also operate in zones A & C).
- _American Deployment_—The Allied player places the 4 American beachhead markers in the landing boxes for **zone A**. American units may only operate in zones A & C unless released from boundary restrictions (allowing them to also operate in zone D).

## 22.26 The Alternate Landing Plan

- _British Deployment_—The Allied player chooses any five consecutive landing boxes in and across **zones A and/or B** and places the 5 British beachhead markers in them. British units may only operate in zones A & B unless released from boundary restrictions (allowing them to also operate in zone C).
- _American Deployment_—The Allied player chooses any four consecutive landing boxes in **zone D** and places the 4 American beachhead markers in them. American units may only operate in zones C&D unless released from boundary restrictions (allowing them to also operate in zone A).

## 22.27 Special Scenario Rules

- _Rules_—All Series and Exclusive Rules are in effect thus all areas, zones and boundaries are in effect.
- _Turn 1 Sequence Exception_—The scenario begins with the Allied Operational Movement Phase skipping the Allied Admin Phase. All other player turns proceed according to the sequence of play.
- _Reinforcements_—All scheduled units, assets and events listed on the Reinforcement reference cards for both players are available and arrive at full strength.

## 22.28 Victory

If a Sudden Death Victory was not achieved, then the VPs are calculated per 19.3 and the final tally is compared to the following table:

- **Axis 4 or more VPs** = Axis win
- **Axis 3 or less VPs** = Allied win

## 22.29 Historical Outcome

After the initial landings, the American landing zones faced ferocious counterattacks by mobile German units, while the British landing zones gained a better footing at first. Over the following nine days, the balance of difficulty switched sides, with the British 8th Army facing a much greater resistance before them, whereas the American 7th Army, ordered to change its direction, advanced rapidly westward.

## 22.3 The Campaign Scenario

## 22.31 Overview

This is the long game, representing the period from the landings on July 10, 1943 through July 31, 1943. Playing this game to completion should take experienced players four to five hours. This is the scenario that gives the players the maximum scope for exploring various landing plans and defensive deployments.

## 22.32 Scenario Length

Play all 9 Game Turns unless a Sudden Death Victory is achieved.

## 22.33 Area of Play

The entire map is used. All land areas of Sicily are Axis-controlled.

## 22.34 Deployment at Start

This is identical to the Tournament Scenario.

## 22.35 The Historical Landing Plan

This is identical to the Tournament Scenario.

## 22.36 The Alternate Landing Plan

This is identical to the Tournament Scenario.

## 22.37 Special Scenario Rules

- _Rules_—All Series and Exclusive Rules are in effect thus all areas, zones and boundaries are in effect.
- _Turn 1 Sequence Exception_—The scenario begins with the Allied Operational Movement Phase skipping the Allied Admin Phase. All other player turns proceed according to the sequence of play.
- _Reinforcements_—All scheduled units, assets and events listed on the Reinforcement reference cards for both players are available and arrive at full strength.
- **Special Historical Scenario Option**: Players desiring to play a strictly historical version of the campaign use the Tournament Scenario deployment rules (22.24) with the following alterations.
- Italian Coastal Garrisons (29 in total) are still shuffled face down as described in 22.24, but may only be placed into the following areas: (a) one unit in each of the 23 invasion-capable coastal areas and (b) one unit in coastal areas 26, 28, 29, 30, 48 and 49.
- The Allied player uses the Historical Landing Plan as described in 22.25 with one additional restriction: the British beachheads must be boxes B1 through B5.

## 22.38 Victory

If a Sudden Death Victory was not achieved, then the VP’s are calculated per 19.3 and the final tally is compared to the following table:

- **4+ VPs** = Operational Victory for the side with the VP’s
- **1-3 VPs** = Marginal Victory for the side with the VP’s
- **0 VPs** = Draw

## 22.39 Historical Outcome

By the end of July, it was clear to the combatants that Operation Husky, the largest amphibious invasion ever undertaken, had proven that the Allied partners could effectively conduct a massive combined operation under extreme circumstances (beach landings into difficult mountainous terrain). The pathway via the “underbelly” looked very bright for the foreseeable future, despite missed opportunities and some brilliant defensive maneuvering.

### Example 1 — Beachhead Selection & Setup

**Overview—** We start with beachhead selection because this is fundamental to understanding the challenge of this design. Around the coast of the island of Sicily there are three ranges of landing boxes pointing to land areas, representing the places Allied planners considered for invasion sites. The historic sites (A1 through A4 and B1 through B6) have specific codenames associated with each of the historic landing sites. The sites labeled D1 through D13 do not have codenames but that entire stretch was considered during Allied planning. In this design, the Axis player does not know whether the Allied player will select the historic plan or some alternate plan. His challenge is to face this unknown. The Allied player will have enough intelligence to see generally where the Axis player has deployed and can ponder his options. The attractions for a historic approach or an alternate approach are different. Which will you pick?

**A—** The players have chosen to play the Campaign Scenario, which allows the Allied player to pick either the Historical Landing Plan or the Alternate Landing Plan. The Allied player chooses the latter, which permits him to land 7th Army (US) forces in any four contiguous landing boxes in Zone D. This represents the selected alternative invasion site for the US forces. Each landing box points to one specific land area on the island of Sicily. During the Reinforcement Phase, the player will place any arriving US units into designated landing boxes (or beachheads). During the Movement Phase, units in beachheads advance (consuming their entire movement point allowance) to the directly connected land areas.

**B—** There are four port boxes on the map, two subject to British use – Syracuse [P1] and Catania [P2], one subject to US use – Palermo [P3] and one subject to German use – Messina [P4]. They cannot be used as beachheads and so cannot be marked by a beachhead marker. Note that a port is an area that can be entered from either a designated landing box (beachhead) or a port box. Once the port of Palermo [57] is Allied-controlled and uncontested, and the Palermo port box [P3] is fully operational (no damage), then the Allied player can place arriving US reinforcements into the Palermo port box [P3]. During the Movement Phase, units in port boxes not only move directly to the port area but may continue moving.

**C—** During scenario preparation, after the Allied player selects the landing plan (per scenario instructions), then he places beachhead markers into specific landing boxes in the appropriate zones. There are thirteen landing boxes in Zone D for the Allied player to choose from. In this case, he decides to place markers in landing boxes D2 through D5, giving him direct access to Palermo [57] and adjacent access to Marsala [65]. Once placed, the beachhead markers cannot be moved, although they can be captured (and permanently eliminated) by the Axis player.

**D—** Once a beachhead marker is placed into a landing box, the beachhead is formally created. It is now ready to support unit arrival. During the Reinforcement Phase, arriving units are placed into available and appropriate beachheads. During the immediately ensuing Movement Phase, units in beachheads are directly advanced from the landing box to the directly connected land area. This process of arrival and advancement to a land area is identical for port boxes, once the proper conditions for control are in place.

**E—** As part of his defensive planning, the Axis player has to decide how to place the many weak Italian Coastal Garrison units (with entry notation S*). A handful of them will put up enough resistance to cause some steps of damage to the Allied invader, but neither player knows which will actually do so. Because of this, the Axis player places all of them face down, randomly shuffles them, and then places them (per the scenario directions) into desired areas across the island. If he places them in an invasion-capable area (one that is pointed to by a landing box), he can beef them up with a field works marker at start. Does he place them at the water, in poor terrain, with a field works marker likely to be nullified by Allied Ranger or Naval Support assets or does he place them inland in better terrain, while yielding to his opponent an easy landing?

**F—** As part of setup, the Axis player also places specific Axis units in predetermined areas (per scenario directions). These units have the area number with yellow highlight as the entry notation. Unlike the coastal garrisons (which represent a practically unknown force), the primary, organized units of the Italian 6th Army were disposed in locations that Allied reconnaissance had mostly determined prior to the invasion. The flexibility of the coastal garrison deployment, therefore, restores to the Axis player more of a chance to induce some doubt in the Allied player’s decision as to where to land.

> Figure: Western Sicily from Marsala [65] to Termini Imerese, with US beachhead markers in landing boxes D2 through D5 (A Landing Box, C Beachhead Marker, D Beachhead at D4), the Palermo port box [P3] “Allied Control Required (7th Army only)” (B Port Box), face-down coastal garrisons with field works markers in coastal areas (E), and an Italian unit set up in its predetermined area beside a garrison (F).

### Example 2 — Draw of Assets & Events

**Overview—** Units, assets and events arrive during the Reinforcement Phase, which happens only at the start of the Game Turn. Both players receive units, assets and events (with a turn of arrival notation, such as **3** for entry on Turn 3). Aside from units already in place at the start of the game, Axis units arrive via the Messina port box [P4]. Allied units must always arrive via beachheads or controlled, undamaged port boxes. Assets and events are placed directly into each player’s Selection Cup, which by play convention are opaque cups (coffee cups, paper cups and so on). Although players know what resources are scheduled to arrive (according to plan), they may only immediately receive some of what they expected (requisitions denied or resources lost in transit). At the beginning of the Admin Phase, both players draw a certain number of counters from their own Selection Cups as specified for that turn. For example, on Turn 3, during the Admin Phase of the Allied player turn, the Allied player may draw 11 counters, while the Axis player may draw only 10 counters. Later during the Admin Phase of the Axis player turn, both players may draw 5 counters. Only after the counters are drawn will the players see what assets and events they have actually received.

**A—** Both players have a Resources section on the map particular to their needs. Certain parts of the sequence of play are listed in the various boxes with a numbered sequence (so the players can perform the necessary steps in order without referring to the player aids). First, the Allied player reviews what he must do in the Reinforcement Phase (using Turn 3 in this example). He sees that he has the Reusable Special Action in the Available Box (step 1 in the Available Box), which allows him to immediately retrieve a used asset from the Used Box. Unless the Allied player saves the Naval Support asset, it must be eliminated (step 3 in the Used Box). He retrieves the Naval Support asset.

**B—** The listed steps (2 and 3) in the Used Box now no longer apply: the Reusable Action was already in the Available Box and no Naval Support assets are in the Used Box (with one subject to elimination). Next, the Allied player places all the remaining used assets into the Selection Cup (step 4 in the Used Box). Back in the Reinforcement Box, step 5 directs the Allied player to place all the assets and events arriving for the turn (in this case, Turn 3) into the Selection Cup as well.

> Figure: The Allied Resources section of the map (Available Box, Used Box, Eliminated Box) with their numbered Reinforcement Phase steps; the Naval Support asset is moved from the Used Box to the Available Box (A) and the remaining used assets are put into the Selection Cup (B).

> Figure: The Allied Resources section after the draw: the counters drawn from the Selection Cup in the Available Box (C) and the additional US 7th Army Release event (+1).

**C—** After both players have completed what they must do in the Reinforcement Phase, it is now the Admin Phase, starting with the Allied player. He looks at the Available Box and sees he is directed to draw counters from the Selection Cup. According to the calendar on the Turn Track, he may draw 11 counters from the Selection Cup, placing them in the Available Box. In addition, since he controls and occupies the undamaged port of Syracuse [5], he may draw one additional asset, the US 7th Army Release event (giving good fortune for the Allied player). He drew no further Naval Support assets, so it turns out that saving the Naval Support asset was a good choice.

**Asset and Event Play—** After drawing the scheduled number of assets and events, the Allied player can now play certain assets and events as desired. The Action Matrix and the Event Summary Table are organized by phase to remind players of the choices they can make with their resources. The Asset Capability Matrix provides even more detail about particular effects of asset play, especially decomposing their use in the Combat Phase. Aside from the ever-valuable Reusable Special Action, two excellent choices for the Allied player would be (i) the possible play of the 3rd CDO asset as a Flanking Invasion in Catania [26] or Lentini [14] to maintain maximum disruption at a crucial defensive point for the Axis player and (ii) the definite play of the US 7th Army Release event, giving immediate breathing room to the scope of Allied advance.

### Example 3 — Air Interdiction & Dogfighting

**Overview—** Air Support assets can be played in the Admin Phase (Interdiction mission), Combat Phase (Ground Support mission) or Supply Phase (Air Supply mission) or by the other player (Dogfighting mission) to immediately cancel the attempted mission in each of those phases.

**A—** During the Axis Player Turn, the Axis player intends to moves his major units on the western end of the island eastwards to respond to the Allied invasion. After the Axis portion of the Admin Phase, the Allied player (during his portion of the Admin Phase) commits Air Support assets to the Interdiction mission to stall the Axis response to Allied progress. He takes two Air Support assets from the Available Box and places one each in Bolognetta [56] and Salemi [59]. Each Allied Air Support asset used for Interdiction will cause any Axis units to pay +1 MP to enter and +1 MP to leave the interdicted area.

**B—** Although it is no longer the Axis portion of the Admin Phase, either player may commit Air Support assets to the Dogfighting mission, which enables that player to cancel one enemy Air Support mission per asset played. The Axis player realizes that his amount of Air Support is much less than for the Allied player, however, he is not willing to have all his units stalled due to Interdiction. He immediately commits his one available Air Support asset to the Dogfighting mission in Salemi [59], which cancels the Allied Air Interdiction mission. This restores freedom of movement to the units in Salemi [59]. Since he does not cancel the Interdiction mission in Bolognetta [56], that mission succeeds and persists until the Axis Supply Phase, when the asset is removed to the Allied Used Box.

**C—** In the Axis Movement Phase, the Axis player is faced with a less than ideal situation. Assume in these examples that either moving unit is not in the way of the other unit (to avoid explaining potential congestion penalties). The Italian unit in Palermo [57] cannot get past Bolognetta [56] since it has only 3 MP to expend: 1 MP along the road plus 1 MP for Interdiction and it cannot leave the area because it must spend at least 2 more MP to leave. At best, it can make its way to Corleone [54] via Rocca Busambra [55]. The Italian unit in Alcamo [58] faces the same problem and cannot go farther than either Bolognetta [56] or Corleone [54]. The Axis player decided to protect his more mobile German armor units in Salemi [59]. These units can take the road south to Castelvetrano [60] and thence along the coast to reach either Canicatti [40] or Palma di Montechiaro [41], moving significantly towards the action on the eastern end of the island. Alternatively, they could reach Caltanissetta [45] via legal road movement using Corleone [54], Prizzi [50], Collesano [48] and Castelbuono [46] (since primary roads connecting adjacent areas do not have to be literally connected to each other inside an area).

> Figure: Western Sicily around Palermo [57]: Allied Air Support assets placed for Interdiction in Salemi [59] and Bolognetta [56] (A), the Axis Air Support asset sent Dogfighting into Salemi [59], cancelling that Interdiction (B), and the Italian units in Palermo [57] and Alcamo [58] moving with their MP costs (1 MP, 2 MP, 3 MP, STOP at Bolognetta), while the German armor in Salemi [59] heads south and east.

### Example 4 — Port Damage & Repair

**A—** During the Reinforcement Phase of Game Turn 3, the Allied player has a US unit arriving, one of the two US 9th Infantry blocks. The battle for Palermo [57] is concluded, with a badly battered 1st Infantry block remaining in the area as a garrison. While the Allied player now controls Palermo [57] because an Allied unit was the last sole occupant of the area, he cannot yet use the Palermo port box [P3] because the port itself (as represented by the port box) is damaged. The consequence for the Allied player is that the sorely needed reinforcement can only be advanced from the beachhead [D2] to Palermo [57] as its sole action during the Movement Phase.

**B—** During the Allied portion of the Admin Phase, the Allied player decides to use an available Engineer asset to repair the port box one level (from Damage 2 to Damage 1). A necessary condition for the play of this asset is that the port area (Palermo [57] itself) must be friendly-controlled, in supply and (for the Allied player) occupied by a US unit. Since Palermo [57] is friendly-controlled, in supply and occupied by the US 1st Infantry, all three conditions are in place for the Allied player to play the Engineer asset to make the repair of the Palermo port box [P3].

**C—** The play of the Engineer asset causes the Damage 2 marker to be flipped to its Damage 1 side. The port box [P3] itself continues to remain unusable, and will remain that way unless and until the Allied player plays yet another available Engineer asset during an Admin Phase that will remove the Damage 1 marker. The Axis player may not, during the ensuing Axis portion of the Admin Phase, play an available Engineer asset to cause the damage to go back up to Damage 2. This is because Palermo [57] is not friendly-controlled and in supply (from the Axis perspective). Were the Axis player able to battle back to Palermo [57], solely occupy it and thereby control it, he could then (in an ensuing Admin Phase) damage the port box [P3] once again.

> Figure: Palermo [57] and the Palermo port box [P3] “Allied Control Required (7th Army only)”: the arriving US unit is placed in the US beachhead in landing box D2 (A), the 540th Engineer asset is played on the port box (B), and the Damage 2 marker on the port box is flipped to Damage 1 (C).

### Example 5 — Army Boundaries

**Overview—** The Allied army is divided into two higher echelons, the 8th Army (British) and the 7th Army (US), which must operate together within some challenging boundary restrictions. The areas within an army’s boundary are collectively referred to by a zone ID. The zones are not strictly fixed, however, since a key to the game focuses on which landing plan the Allied player chooses (based on the Axis disposition). For example, when the Allied player uses the Historical Landing Plan, the British forces must land and operate in Zone B and the US forces must land and operate in Zones A and C (see the map). Zone D represents another operating area, which the forces may move into when formally released (via special actions or events). If the Allied player uses the Alternate Landing Plan, British forces can land and operate in both Zones B & A, whereas the US forces must land in Zone D and may operate in Zones D and C. When released, British forces may additionally operate in Zone C, whereas US forces may additionally operate in Zone A. In all cases, no US force may ever operate in Zone B and no British force may ever operate in Zone D. The scenarios outline the specific conditions depending on the selected landing plan. For clarity, all the components of this example assume the Historical Landing Plan boundary definitions.

> Figure: Central Sicily with the Zone A / Zone C boundary: the British 5th Infantry in Mount Etna [27] (A), the British 51st Infantry in Catania [26] (B), the US 2nd Armor in Leonforte [24] (C), the US 1st Infantry in Enna [38] (D), the 1st Canadian in Mazzarino [39] (E) and the US 45th Infantry in Ramacca [16] (F), each with its divisional release marker and its possible moves marked “Yes”, “NO” or “Never”.

**A—** The British 5th Infantry in Mount Etna [27] is currently operating in Zone B. The divisional release marker in the 8th Army Boundary Restrictions box indicates that the unit is restricted (for clarity, the appropriate divisional release markers are placed on the map next to the designated units). In this state, the unit may not yet move into Adrano [33] in Zone C, nor may it yet move to Regalbuto [25] in Zone A. When released, both moves will become legal.

**B—** The British 51st Infantry in Catania [26] is currently operating in Zone B. The divisional release marker indicates that the unit is released. In this state, the unit may move to Regalbuto [25] in Zone A and may continue on to Adrano [33] in Zone C.

**C—** The US 2nd Infantry in Leonforte [24] is currently operating in Zone A. The divisional release marker indicates that the unit is restricted. In this state, the unit may already move into Nicosia [35] in Zone C, but it may not yet move to Caltanissetta [45] in Zone D. When released, the latter move will become legal.

**D—** The US 1st Infantry in Enna [38] is currently operating in Zone A. The divisional release marker indicates that the unit is released. In this state, the unit may already move to Gangi [37] in Zone C and it may now move to Castelbuono [46] in Zone D.

**E—** The 1st Canadian Infantry (8th Army) in Mazzarino [39] is currently operating in Zone A. The divisional release marker indicates that the unit is released. In this state, it may operate throughout Zones A, B and C, but it may never voluntarily enter Zone D (the 8th Army is prohibited from operating in that zone).

**F—** The US 45th Infantry in Ramacca [16] is currently operating in Zone A. The divisional release marker indicates that the unit is restricted. In this state, it may only operate in Zone A. When released, it may then additionally operate in Zones C and D, but it may never voluntarily enter Zone A (the 7th Army is prohibited from operating in that zone).

**Release—** Release from army boundary restrictions is accomplished in the following ways: (1) The 7th Army can be entirely released by the play of the 7th Army Release event, or individual 7th Army formations can be released via play of a Special Action and (2) The 8th Army can **only** be released formation by formation via play of a Special Action. Note that when you release an 8th Army division, you can simultaneously release one of the three independent Brigades (1st Canadian, 4th Armour, and 231st Infantry).

**Retreat—** While you may not voluntarily violate an army boundary restriction, it is possible that there is no other way to properly conduct a retreat. In such a case, it depends on whether the unit is forced across a “restricted” army boundary (in which case, it will become released to operate in the eligible zone) or across a “prohibited” army boundary (in which case, it must attempt to return as quickly and directly as possible to an eligible zone).

### Example 6 — Basic Movement

**Movement and Boundaries—** There are two standard boundary types in the game: open and river. River boundaries always have bridges. When units move across the boundaries, the type of connection they use — road or field — defines the movement cost. When a primary road crosses the boundary of an area into another area — whether the boundary is open or river — that makes the connection a **road** connection. Otherwise, that connection — whether the boundary is open or river — is a **field** connection. Note that the road depictions inside an area do not have to connect with one another.

**A—** The mechanized German 15th PG unit moves south into Monti Sicani [51], costing 1 MP for the road connection. The unrevealed Italian Untried unit does not impose a penalty on moving Axis units for congestion (since it is Untried, it is unclear whether it will function and to what degree). Then it moves into Aragona [43], costing an additional 2 MP for the field connection, and on to Canicatti [40] for an additional 2 MP for the field connection for a total of 5 MP spent. It cannot enter Caltanissetta [45] to attack the US 9th Infantry unit since it needs two more MP to do it: 1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area.

**B—** The mechanized unit moves into Mussomeli [44], costing 3MP (2 MP for the field connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area), and on to Castelbuono [46], costing an additional 1 MP only for the road connection (no congestion penalty applies due to the unrevealed Italian Untried unit). Because the congestion penalty does not apply, the mechanized unit has enough MP to enter Caltanissetta [45] to attack the US unit: 1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area.

**C—** The mechanized unit swings northward into Collesano [48], costing 1 MP for the road connection, then on to Castelbuono [46], costing an additional 1 MP only for the road connection (no congestion penalty applies due to the unrevealed Italian Untried unit). Note that primary roads connecting adjacent areas do not have to be literally connected to each other inside an area (a viable road network is assumed in these particular cases). The mechanized unit can not enter Caltanissetta [45] to attack the US unit: 1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area. With a total of 4 MP spent, this is clearly the fastest way to reach the target area.

**D—** The motorized Italian Livorno unit moves directly ahead into Mussomeli [44], costing 3 MP (2 MP for the field connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area). It must stop for two reasons: (1) No unit may cross a blown bridge unless it started in the area with the boundary marked by the blown bridge (that is, “directly adjacent”), thus, the unit cannot enter into Caltanissetta [45]; (2) The motorized Italian Livorno division (both blocks) normally has an MP allowance of 3, but this can be increased to 6 if during the entire MP expenditure it neither leaves nor enters an enemy-occupied area; it therefore cannot seek to use this additional movement capability to enter the target area since this would negate the availability of the additional MP.

**E—** The motorized unit can move into Mussomeli [44], costing 3 MP and continue on to Castelbuono [46], costing an additional 1 MP only, leaving potentially 2 MP to spend (using its motorized capability). However, it is not permitted to enter the enemy-occupied Caltanissetta [45] when using the motorized movement bonus, and thus it must stop. It might continue eastwards down the road, or to unoccupied Enna [38] for its final 2 MP (motorized).

**F—** The motorized unit can also swing northward into Collesano [48], costing 1 MP for the road connection, then on to Castelbuono [46], costing an additional 1 MP only for the road connection (no congestion penalty applies due to the unrevealed Italian Untried unit). But it still cannot enter Caltanissetta [45] since to enter will require an additional 2 MP (1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area). It cannot reach the target area whether using its infantry mode (3 MP) or its motorized mode (6 MP).

**G—** Italian Untried units are severely restricted in terms of what they can do. Only one Untried unit can do anything other than hold position during an Operational Movement Phase. Otherwise, Untried units remain where originally placed unless (i) one of them is selected to “do something” (move, build field works or be placed into reserve) or (ii) Allied units “try” them in combat by entering the area (and they survive).

### Example 7 — Restricted Terrain Boundaries

**Overview—** The area of Mount Etna [27] is bounded on all accessible sides by the restricted terrain boundary depiction. Infantry class units must expend their entire movement allowance to cross that boundary. Armor class units are strictly prohibited from crossing the boundary. The exception to these restrictions is that the side that controls the area enables friendly units to use road connections into and out of the area.

**A—** The armor class German 15th PG in Catania [26] can enter Mount Etna [27] costing 1 MP for the road connection, +1 MP to exit a contested area and +1 MP to enter an occupied area. The unit can also use road connections to pass through the area. It enters Mount Etna [27] for 3 MP, then crosses over to Adrano [33] costing 1 MP for the road connection and stops. It may not enter into either Regalbuto [25] or Randazzo [32] even though it has sufficient MP available since a unit cannot leave a contested area and then enter or create another contested area in the same move.

**B—** The armor class British 4th Armour in Regalbuto [25] can enter Catania [26] costing 2 MP for the field connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area. It cannot enter Mount Etna [27] since this area is under Axis control. It may participate in an end run around the volcano by entering Adrano [33] costing 1 MP for the road connection, then to Randazzo [32] costing 1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area, and finally into Novara [30] costing 1 MP for the road connection and +1MP to enter an occupied area for a total of 5 MP.

**C—** The infantry class British 51st Infantry in Randazzo [32] can enter Mount Aetna [27] despite the move costing more than its movement allowance of 3 MP (All MP to cross the restricted boundary +1 MP to enter an occupied area). It is not prohibited from crossing (infantry class) and therefore it can use Guaranteed Minimum Movement to move to the adjacent area.

**D—** The armor class German 15th PG in Novara [30] is prohibited from directly entering Mount Etna [27] by crossing the restricted boundary, but it may use the roads to get there, first by entering Taormina [28] costing 1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area, and then into Mount Etna [27] for 1 MP (road) and +1 MP to enter an occupied area for a total of 4 MP.

**E—** The armor class German 29th PG in Taormina [28] can enter Mount Etna [27] for 2 MP (1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area) and may continue through the area and exit on road connections. It could move on to Catania [26] for an additional 2 MP (1 MP for the road connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area) or move out to Adrano [33] for 1 MP (road connection) and end up in Regalbuto [25] to combat the British 4th Armour, costing an additional 2 MP (road connection and entry into occupied area) for a total of 5 MP.

**F—** The infantry class Italian Napoli Infantry in Mount Aetna [27] is eligible to exit the area using the road connections or the field connections (requiring use of all MP).

### Example 8 — Bridge Crossing

**A—** During the Axis portion of the Admin Phase, the Axis player examined bn the front line situation to decide whether to place an available Engineer in friendly-occupied Val di Catania [15] to automatically blow the bridges (on the Anapo River) to Sortino [12] and Lentini [14]. Neither side has many engineer assets, so perhaps he decided to hold on to the precious engineer asset for use along the Simeto River bordering Catania [26].

**B—** During the Operational Movement Phase, the Allied player attempts to move the 1st Canadian Infantry from Sortino [12] into Val di Catania [15] across the river boundary (for a possible cost of 2MP for the field connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area). At the instant he declares this attempt, the Axis player, given that this is the first movement attempt across the river boundary, may attempt to blow the bridge. The Success Number (SN) for the attempt is 5 (no modifications allowed) and the Axis player rolls 4 — success! He places the Blown Bridge marker immediately. Now the Allied player must decide whether to continue crossing (at the cost of All MP) or continue moving elsewhere (not yet having formally spent any MP of his allowance of 3 MP). Given how congested the situation is, he will not be able to get this unit into Val di Catania [15] unless he crosses this boundary. Therefore, he moves the unit across for All MP. He now places a River Assault marker into the area, since (at this moment) all units moving into the area (one of them) have crossed a river boundary, one of which must have a blown bridge (which is the case).

**C—** One of the British 50th Infantry units in Lentini [14] could attempt to cross the Anapo River boundary into Val di Catania [15]. This would be the first attempt to cross that boundary, so the Axis player could try to blow the bridge. Note that even if the attempt to blow the bridge fails, and the infantry unit enters the area across that boundary, the River Assault condition will still apply.

**D—** If the British 1st Cdn Infantry unit in Sortino [11] moves into Val di Catania [15] instead (for 2 MP for field connection and +1 MP to enter an occupied area), the River Assault condition will no longer apply. It can be difficult to crack a defensive position along a river line, so this move from Sortino [11] would actually be the better move for the Allied player. On top of taking away the River Assault, both blocks of the same division (1st Cdn Infantry) would be in the same area and so would receive the Divisional Integrity bonus (plus one die) in combat.
