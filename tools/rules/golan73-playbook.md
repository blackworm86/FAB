## 20.0 Designer’s Notes

I started working on _FAB: Golan ’73_ back in 2009 after becoming interested in this conflict. I wanted to have a game on the subject and after playing _FAB: The Bulge_ I was thinking “hey, this system would be perfect for a game on the battle for Golan.” So, I started working on the OOB, which was very difficult since all the sources varied so much. Both the Syrians and the Israelis are very careful and restrictive about information on the forces involved and I had to change the OOB several times. I’m proud of the final result and I think it’s one of the best OOB in games covering the battle. The scale of using brigades was pretty easy, however, to reflect the superior Israeli combat strength and their flexibility I decided to have each brigade representing two units. Syrian brigades are represented by one unit. So each pip represents one battalion for the Syrians and about a half battalion for the Israelis.

The most challenging design issue with this game was the map[?;?] the northern Golan Heights area is very poor tank country as it’s filled with lava rocks and slopes. The southern end on the other hand is pretty clear terrain and more suitable for mechanized warfare. There are also lots of “tels” (lava hills) spread over the Golan Heights which affects combat across the region.

I struggled with the design for some time and eventually I asked Rick if he was interested to join me as a co-designer. After that the game really took a boost. I must admit, if it wasn’t for him, I doubt the game would ever have been published! During the work we had a very good dialogue and we shared many ideas and thoughts. I really hope that the players will enjoy _FAB: Golan ’73_ and I also hope we see lots of more volumes in the Fast Action Battle Series.

— _Michael_

## 21.0 Player’s Notes

## 21.1 Both Players

In _FAB: Golan ’73_ the emphasis is on mechanized warfare; there are more armor-class units than infantry-class units. As a comparison to the previous two volumes we look at this:

| Unit Class | _The Bulge_ (Volume) | _Sicily_ (Volume) | _Golan’73_ (Volume) |

The unit mix is mostly mechanized, but the terrain is not at all favorable to a lightning strike. The mobile capability will still prove useful to rush reserves to the decision point.

Familiarize yourself with the assets and events before the game. There are some more modern elements in this game compared to the WW II volumes of _FAB_, with Electronic Warfare events and Surface-to-Air Missiles being a few to become very familiar with.

Have fun with this one. It is a tense situation and a great game to enjoy!

— _Rick & Michael_

## 21.2 Syrian Player

Your job is to stretch the Israeli defense to the breaking point, and then break it. You cannot take a slow and steady approach, or try to hit at a single point with everything. You have to hit just about everywhere and tax the Israeli ability to keep a solid line. Yes, losses will be heavy, but if the line does snap, you should be able to grab the real estate you need to win, and then shift over to the defensive and let the Israeli player worry about the terrain for a change.

## 21.3 Israeli Player

It will be difficult at times, but you need to keep your line stitched together and give ground as needed to keep your units alive. Reinforcements, good reinforcements, are coming, and when they arrive it will be your troops launching the offensive. Hold on until the help arrives (easier said than done).

## 22.11 Overview

This is the standard, historical scenario, representing the period from the initial assault by the Syrians on October 6, 1973 through October 11, 1973. The Syrian player must press the attack as strongly as possible before the powerful Israeli reserves arrive.

Play all 9 Game Turns unless a Sudden Death Victory is achieved.

If a Sudden Death Victory was not achieved, then the VP’s are calculated per 19.3 and the final tally is compared to the following table:

- **3+ VPs** = Operational Victory for the side with the VP’s
- **1-2 VPs** = Marginal Victory for the side with the VP’s
- **0 VPs** = Draw

## 22.19 Historical Outcome

The Yom Kippur War opened on October 6, 1973, with coordinated operations by Egypt in the Sinai and by Syria in the Golan Heights. Syrian forces used a combination of air-mobile and missile protection for their mechanized forces and overran much of the southern Golan reaching their furthest extent by October 7. Starting October 8, counterattacking Israeli units began to push Syrian forces back and had regained the Purple Line position by October 10.

### Example 1 — Draw of Assets & Events

**Overview—** Units, assets and events arrive during the Reinforcement Phase, which happens only at the start of the Game Turn. Both players receive units, assets and events (with a turn of arrival notation, such as 3 for entry on Turn 3). Aside from units already in place at the start of the game. Syrian units arrive at entry points V-Z. Israeli units must arrive via Assembly areas A-B. Assets and events are placed directly into each player's Selection Cup, which by play convention are opaque cups (coffee cups, paper cup and so on). Although players know what resources are scheduled to arrive (according to plan), they may only immediately receive some of what they expected (requisitions denied or resources lost in transit). At the beginning of the Admin Phase, both players draw a certain number of counters from their own Selection Cups as specified for that turn on the Turn Track on the map. For example, on Turn 4, during the Admin Phase of the Syrian player turn, the Syrian player may draw 6 counters, while the Israeli player may draw only 3 counters. Later during the Admin Phase of the Israeli player turn, the Israeli player may draw 4 counters and the Syrian player may draw 2 counters. Only after the counters are drawn will the players see what assets and events they have actually received.

**A—** Both players have a Resources section on the map particular to their needs. Certain parts of the sequence of play are listed in the various boxes with a numbered sequence (so the players can perform the necessary steps in order without referring to the player aids). First, the Syrian player reviews what he must do in the Reinforcement Phase (using Turn 4 in this example). He sees that he has the Reusable Special Action in the Available Box (step 1 in the Available Box), which allows him to immediately retrieve a used asset from the Used Box. The Syrian player retrieves the Air Support asset.

**B—** The listed step 2 no longer applies: the Reuasble Special Action was already in the Available Box. In step 3, the Syrian player rolls one die for each of the two SAM assets and he rolls an 8 and a 4. As a result, one SAM asset is moved to the Eliminated Box.

**C—** In step 4 the Syrian player places the remaining used assets into the Selection Cup. Back in the Reinforcement Box, step 5 directs the Syrian player to place all the assets and events arriving for the turn (in this case, Turn 4) into the Selection Cup as well.

**D—** After both players have completed what they must do in the Reinforcement Phase, it is now the Admin Phase, starting with the Syrian player. He looks at the Available Box and sees he is directed to draw counters from the Selection Cup. According to the calendar on the Turn Track, he may draw 6 counters from the Selection Cup, placing them in the Available Box.

Assets and Event Play – After drawing the scheduled number of assets and events, the Syrian player can now play certain assets and events as desired. The Action Matrix and the Event Summary Table (on a player aid) are organized by phase to remind players of the choices they can make with their resources. The Asset Capability Matrix provides even more detail about particular effects of asset play, especially decomposing their use in the Combat Phase.

### Example 2 — Air Interdiction & SAM Air Defense

**Overview—** Air support assets can be played in the Admin Phase (Interdiction and Aerial Reconnaissance missions) and in the Combat Phase (Ground Support mission).

**A—** During the Syrian Player Turn, the Syrian player intends to move his 1st Tank Division units towards the frontline at the Golan Heights to support the breakthrough made by the 9th Infantry Division in the Central Sector. After the Syrian portion of the Admin Phase, the Israeli player (during his portion of the Admin Phase) commits Air Support assets to the Interdiction mission to stall the advance of the 1st Tank Division. He takes two Air Support asset from the Available Box and places one each in Jasim [75] and Tel Kudne [73]. Each Israeli Air Support asset used for Interdiction will cause any Syrian units to pay +1 MP to enter and +1 MP to leave the interdicted area.

**B—** Although it is no longer the Syrian portion of the Admin Phase, he may commit SAM assets in response to the Israeli Interdiction mission, which enables the Syrian player to cancel one enemy Air Support mission per asset played. He immediately commits two SAM assets and places one each in Jasim [75] and Tel Kudne [73] and rolls one die for each of the SAM assets commited. He first rolls a die for the SAM asset in Jasim [75], a 3 is rolled, he checks the Air Defense Fire Table (under Turn 4-6 column) and the result is "Air Asset to Used Box". The Israeli player moves the air asset to the Used Box and the Syrian does likewise with his SAM asset. He then rolls for the SAM asset in Tel Kudne [73], a 6 is rolled, he checks the Air Defense Fire Table and the result is "No Effect". He moves the SAM asset to the Used Box. The Israeli Air Support asset remains in the area until his own Supply Phase.

**C—** In the Syrian Movement Phase, the Syrian player is faced with a less than ideal situation. He wants to reach the battle with his 1st Tank Division units on the Golan as fast as possible, before Israeli reinforcements starts to arrive en masse, but now he will be slowed down due to the Interdiction mission at Tel Kudne [73]. He first moves the 91st Tank unit, spending 1 MP for the road connection into Swisah [74] and then 2 MP to enter Tel Kudne [73] and additional 2 MP to leave the area due to the Interdiction misson placed by the Israeli player. The same goes with the 58th Mech unit, but that unit must spend 3 MP to leave Tel Kudne [73] due to the Interdiction mission and the area is already occupied by a unit.

### Example 3 — Basic Movement

**Movement and Boundaries—** There are four standard boundaries in the game: open, river, wadi and escarpment (representing the steep cliffs and ravines between the Jordan Valley and the Golan Heights). Unlike the two previous games in the series, bridges only exist where a road crosses a river boundary. When units move across the boundaries, the type of connection they use – road or field – defines the movement cost. When a primary road crosses the boundary of an area into another area – whether the boundary is open, river, wadi or escarpment – that makes the connection a **road** connection. Otherwise, that connection – whether the boundary is open, river, wadi or escarpment – is a **field** connection. Note that the road depictions inside an area do not have to connect with one another.

**A—** The Israeli 4th Mech unit moves east into B'not Yakov Bridge [7], costing 1 MP for the road (bridge) connection and on up the Golan Heights crossing the escarpment boundary into Katzrin [36], costing one additional MP. The unit then moves across the wadi boundary into Sindiana [37] via a field connection paying 3 MP, with a total of 6 MP, the unit must stop there, since 6 MP is the maximum MP armor-class units may spend in a Movement Phase.

**B—** The Israeli 4th Mech unit moves south into Kfar Hanasi [9] costing 3 MP (2 MP for the field connection and +1 to enter an occupied area). The unit may not cross the river boundary into Had Nes [8] since no bridge exists if there is no road connection between areas.

**C—** The Israeli 179th Armored unit moving east across the river boundary via field connection. This move costs the unit its entire movement allowance since no bridge exists on river boundaries if there is no road connection.

**D—** The Israeli 179th Armored moves north into Gadot [6], costs 2 MP and turns east across the river into B'not Yakov Bridge [7], costs 1 MP. The unit cannot cross the escarpment boundary into Katzrin [36] since the boundary has already being crossed by the 4th Mech unit this Movement Phase.

**E—** The Israeli Golani Infantry unit may not cross the escarpment boundary into Had Nes [8] since units may only move across escarpment boundaries across road connections.

**F—** The Israeli Golani Infantry unit moves north across the open boundary via field connection into Katzrin [36], costing 2 MP and must end its movement there since it only have 3 MP to spend as an infantry-class unit. The unit cannot move into Nafakh [42] since the area is occupied (+1 MP) and not across the wadi boundary into Aleika [43] (also +1 MP). The unit cannot enter B'not Yakov Bridge [7] since the escarpment boundary costs +1 MP _and_ that escarpment boundary has already been crossed by another unit (the 4th Mech unit) this Movement Phase. The unit may not enter either Kuzabia [34] or Sindiana [37] since it does not have enough movement points to spend.

**G—** The Israeli Golani Infantry unit moves along the road into Kuzabia [34] costing 2 MP (+1 for being occupied) and further into Hushniyah [38] costs 1 MP for a total of 3 MP maximum to spend.

**H—** The Israeli 53/188th Armored unit in Kuzabia [34] moves into the enemy occupied Zohar [33] area, costs 2 MP (1 MP for the road connection +1 for being occupied), and must stop there since there is an enemy unit in the area.

### Example 4 — Rugged Boundaries

**Overview—** This is an additional boundary type in this game, which represents exceptionally difficult terrain. Without a road connection across this boundary, only infantry-class units may cross this boundary type and must spend their entire movement allowance to move across. The rugged boundary between Mevo Hama [18] and Givat Yoav [19] has a road connection and thus allows armor units to cross (at the road movement cost).

**A—** The Syrian 121st Mech unit in Booster [50] moves west through Merom Golan [51] and into Wasit [52] along the the road spending 2 MP (1 MP each). Then the unit turns southward, crossing the boundary into Tel Abu-Nida [46] (costs 3 MP, 2 for the field connection and +1 for being occupied).

**B—** The Syrian 121st Mech unit moves along the road into Merom Golan [51]. The unit cannot cross the rugged boundary since armor-class units may not move across rugged boundaries (unless there is a road connection).

**C—** The Syrian 85th Infantry unit moves across the rugged boundary into Tel Abu-Nida [46] spending its entire movement allowance.

**D—** The Syrian 85th Infantry unit may not cross the Operational boundary between Kuneitra [49] and Bunker 109 [48] since (assuming it is Turn 3) no unit may cross the operational boundary before Game Turn 5.

### Example 5 — Anti-Tank Ditch Crossing

**Overview—** This is also an additional boundary type in this game. The Anti-Tank Ditch boundary stretches from the southern end of the map to the northern end, along its length is also the Purple Line, which divides the Israeli occupied Golan Heights from Syria. Also, the Israelis had built several strongpoints along the Purple Line as a first defense barrier in order to slow down a Syrian attack against the Golan.

**A—** The Syrian 85th Infantry unit in Khan Arnabe [64] may enter Booster [50] moving across the anti-tank (AT) ditch despite the move costing more than its movement allowance of 3MP (All MP to cross the AT ditch boundary +1 MP to enter an occupied area). The attack is a Crossing Assault (and is marked with the generic River Assault marker) which will have effect in the ensuing Combat Phase. This unit will try to enable a breakthrough to allow following armor and mech units to use the road leading to the Wasit crossroads.

**B—** The Syrian 68th Infantry in Jubata [61] conducts a Crossing Assault by moving across the AT ditch into Tel Hermonit [57]. The unit’s mission is to establish a bridgehead in the area and to throw the Israelis back from this vital area.

**C—** The Syrian Moroccan (Maroc) Infantry unit in Hadar [60] crosses the AT ditch into Majdal Shams [59]. This maneuver is merely a try to force the Israeli player to split his forces since its chances for success are limited.

**D—** The Syrian 7th Armored Recon unit in Horfa [62] may not cross the AT ditch since it costs a unit its entire movement allowance to cross. So the only thing this unit can do is to approach the AT ditch and try to cross it during next or a later turn.

**E—** The Syrian 78th Armored unit in Maaz [63] moves into Khan Arnabe [64] and stops there to await the result of the upcoming combats to see whether a breakthrough opportunity develops.

### Example 6: Minefields

**Overview—** Only the Israeli player has minefields, which are placed (as counters) on boundaries at the start of the game. Minefields are effective to slow the Syrian advance across the Golan and force the Syrian player to use precious engineering assets that could be used elsewhere where needed.

**A—** The Syrian 9th Armored Recon unit tries to cross the minefield between Bunker 109 [48] and Ein Zivan [47]. The Syrian player must make a morale check, rolls the die, a 7 is rolled and it is a failure. The unit may not cross the minefield.

**B—** The Syrian 9th Armored Recon unit must now take another route to reach Ein Zivan [47] by spending a total of 5 MP.

**C—** The Syrian 52nd Infantry unit also tries to cross the minefield, making a morale check and rolls a 4 – success. The 52nd Infantry unit may now enter Ein Zivan [47], at a cost of 3 MP (1 MP for the road connection, +1 for the area being occupied, +1 for the minefield).

### Example 7: Strategic Movement

**Overview—** In _FAB: Golan ’73_ strategic movement is only allowed along road connections due to the terrain. The Golan Plateau was very poor tank country, and, although it was more passable in its southern part than in the north, areas of it were impassable to vehicles because of the huge basalt boulders and outcrops of rock. Several roads crossed the plateau, and there was a network of motorable tracks made by the army and the settlers. Strategic movement is limited to both players by the number of reserve markers per side. Strat-moves allow units to rapidly redeploy elsewhere on the map. Unlike Operational Movement which enables units to move into enemy-controlled areas, Strategic Movement only enables units to move freely within friendly-controlled areas (never crossing a blown bridge nor moving into an area that is enemy-controlled and/or contested). This is the intent for the Israelis since the Tel Faris [30], an important location on the Golan Heights, is being threatened by Syrian forces moving towards the area. The Israeli player places one of the 4th Mech Brigade units located in Assembly Area A in reserve during the Operational Movement Phase. Then in the Strategic Movement Phase, he moves that reserve unit directly to Tel Faris [30]. Once moved, the reserve marker is removed to the Reserve Holding Box.

**Possible Operational Moves—** As a way to compare the value of the strategic move versus the operational move, see how no operational move gets the 4th Mech unit to Tel Faris [30]:

- Possible Op Move: from Assembly Area A to Almagor [10] for 1 MP, to Arik Bridge [11] for 1MP, to Ad Dardarah [12] for 1 MP, up the escarpment to Ma’ale Gamla [24] for 2 MP (1 MP for the road +1 MP for the escarpment), and finally to Yonatan [26] for 1 MP and no more MP available.
- Possible Op Move: from Assembly Area A to Almagor [10] for 1 MP, to Arik Bridge [11] for 1MP, to Ad Dardarah [12] for 1 MP, to Yehudia [35] for 1 MP, to Kuzabia [34] for 1 MP and finally to Hushniya [38] for 1 MP and no more MP available.

### Example 8: Basic Combat

**Overview—** The Phasing player (the Attacker) determines the order in which combats are resolved in contested areas. Combat is mandatory in a newly contested area, but it is optional in a previously contested area — sometimes pinning your opponent is more effective than attacking. When the Phasing player selects a contested area for combat, a single round of combat occurs — defined strictly in 6.2. Avoid the temptation to treat the individual steps in 6.2 as simultaneous; they are not. Note that the hit priorities for the Defender are different than those for the Attacker. Also note that the Hit Priority sequence should be literally followed after _each_ of the four firing segments in a combat (if hits were caused): (i) Attacker artillery fire, (ii) Defender artillery fire, (iii) Defender ground fire and (iv) Attacker ground fire.

**Battle Asset Limitations—** Although players will not usually run into these limits, remember that the Attacker can commit no more than eight battle assets to a combat and that the Defender can commit no more than five battle assets to a combat.

**A—** The Israeli player (Attacker) must assign assets to the battle in Hushniya [38] first with a battle asset (Armor Task Force) and one artillery asset (405th+55th). The Syrian player (Defender) then follows with one artillery asset (89th) and one Air Support asset. Both must simultaneously designate a point unit. The Syrian player has no choice (the 91st Tank) and the Israeli player arbitrarily picks one of the two 4th Mechanized Brigade units. The attacker fires artillery first.

- **405th+55th** has –1 (target’s point unit is armor-class), –1 (target is the defender) for a net Success Number (SN) of 5–1–1=3 and fires with one die. This is expressed as “1 try for a 3” — rolls 2 — one hit!

This is applied immediately. The first hit priority for the Defender is to remove field works if present (this is the case).

**B—** Now the defender fires artillery.

- **89th** has –1 (Target is armor-class) for a net SN of 5–1=4 and fires one die. This is “1 try for 4” — rolls 7 — miss!

After artillery fire, the artillery assets are placed in the Used Box.

**C—** The Defender can now conduct ground fire. The firing order is not important. The Syrian player committed Air Support to the combat. The Ground Support mission can be used in two ways: (i) as an artillery asset or (ii) as a special battle asset, contributing +1 (+2 for Israeli ground support if used) to the SN of one unit. The Syrian player has saved the Air Support for the latter use.

- **91st Tank** has +1 (firer is an armor unit), +1 (ground support mission), –1 (target’s point unit is armor-class) and +1 (elite firing on veteran) for a net SN of 5+1+1–1+1=7. This is “3 tries for a 7” — rolls 2, 3, 8 — two hits!

The Israeli player has two hits to resolve and decides to press the attack — perhaps another lucky roll by the Syrian player for a third hit would have been enough to cause the Israeli player to abort the attack. The first hit is a mandatory step loss, so he eliminates the Armor Task Force asset (placing it into the Eliminated Box). The Israeli player decides not to abort the attack (which would end the combat, disorder both 4th Mech units, and avoid the second step loss). Therefore the second hit must be applied to the designated point unit (one of the 4th Mech units), which absorbs the second hit. If the Syrian player had scored three hits, the other 4th Mech must take the third hit since no unit can take a second hit before all units in the combat take a first hit.

**D—** Now the Israeli player can conduct ground fire. Since the entire brigade is present (both units are denoted by the same color stripe), Brigade Integrity applies: one extra die for the unit with the lower SN at the time of ground fire.

- **First 4th Mech** has +1 (armor-class firing), –1 (target’s point unit is armor-class), +0 (for the Defender’s Terrain Difficulty Number or TDN) and –1 (veteran firing on elite) for a net SN of 5+1–1+0–1=4. Brigade integrity applies to this unit of the brigade (one extra die added to the two dice for the two steps of strength). This is “3 tries for a 4” — rolls 4, 5, 9 — one hit!
- **Second 4th Mech** has +1 (armor-class firing), –1 (target’s point unit is armor-class), +0 (for the Defenders TDN) and –1 (veteran firing on elite) for a net SN of 5+1–1+0–1=4. This is “3 tries for a 4” — rolls 1, 6, 8 — one hit!

**E—** The Syrian player has two hits to resolve. The Air Support asset is not a battle asset, so it may not be eliminated to absorb one of the hits. The first two hit priorities no longer apply (no field works and the hits were not caused by artillery fire). First he retreats the 91st Tank unit to Tel Faris [30] resulting in the unit becoming disordered — this absorbs the first hit. The second hit must be taken as a step loss to the 91st Tank unit.

### Example 9: Crossing Assault

**Overview—** This volume provides three types of assaults as in the previous volume. However, in _FAB: Golan ’73_ the airborne assault is handled different than in _FAB: Sicily_. The standard type of assault, the river assault, remains unmodified. One new type of assault is the crossing assault. Note that when any one of the assault conditions applies, the attack is marked with the generic River Assault marker and the penalty for ground fire applies as well. If more than one type of assault can occur in an area, see 14.01 to determine which type of assault (which combat rules and other conditions) will be used to resolve the combat. Once crossing (in the Operational Movement Phase) is complete, the players conduct the Combat Phase. Except for the air assault (performed first), the Phasing player chooses the order in which the other combats are conducted. During Game Turn 1, all combats are either crossing assaults or air assault (where the Israeli player defend the Purple Line and Mt. Hermon [83]).

**Note on Combat Order—** In the process of resolving a combat, one area is selected by the Attacker, then assets/events are placed (Attacker first), point units are selected (Defender first) and finally units are revealed. The two combats depicted in this example are completely resolved one at a time (not in parallel as narrated below) — it is only a convenience that this example treats them simultaneously.

**Note on Strongpoints—** These special units represents bunkers, minefield, tank ramparts and other various defensive systems the Israelis built along the Purple Line as a first defensive line in order to slow down a Syrian invasion on the Golan Heights. In the game, they are either armor or infantry supported (noted on the unit label (with a tank symbol for armor and a rifle symbol for infantry supported). Strongpoints always fire with one die regardless of “steps” (the steps are merely a way to show the ability to sustain damage to the strongpoint units) and may only fire as the defender (they may not attack). They may not retreat, but they can be evacuated by other units either leaving or retreating from the area containing the strongpoint. See the Combat Table for the different modifiers for the two types of strongpoints.

**A—** In Tel Saki [27] and Juhader [28] the Israeli player has placed one strongpoint each, an armored supported strongpoint in Tel Saki [27] and an infantry supported strongpoint in Juhader [28]. After assets have been allocated, point units selected and units revealed in both combat locations, it is now time for artillery fire. Only the Syrian player has commited artillery assets to the ensuing combats.

- **50th and 89th** have –1 (target is a strongpoint) and –1 (target is the defender) for a net SN of 5–1–1=3 and both fire one die each. This is expressed as “1 try for a 3” — 50th artillery rolls 2 — hit, and 89th artillery rolls 5 — miss!

The Israeli has one hit to resolve in the Tel Saki [27] area against the strongpoint. Since strongpoints may not retreat, he places an Disordered marker on the armor supported strongpoint (the second hit priority applies).

**B—** Now past the artillery fire steps in the combat procedure, it is time for ground fire. We’ll examine ground fire by the Defender in both locations first, followed by ground fire by the Attacker in both locations last. In Juhader [28] the Defender conducts ground fire.

- **Strongpoint** in Juhader [28] has –1 (infantry supported) and +1 (targeting unit(s) marked with a River Assault marker) for a net SN of 5–1+1=5 and fires with one die. This is “1 try for a 5” — rolls 2 — hit!

The Syrian player has one hit to resolve. The Syrian player decides to eliminate the 5th AT asset (placing it in the Eliminated Box).

**C—** In Tel Saki [27] the Defender conducts ground fire as well.

- **Strongpoint** in Tel Saki [27] has –1 (disordered), +1 (armor-supported strongpoint), +1 (targeting unit(s) marked with a River Assault marker) and +1 (for being elite firing at veteran) for a net SN of 5–1+1+1+1=7 and fires with one die. This is “1 try for a 7” — rolls 6 — Hit!

The Syrian player has one hit to resolve. He applies the hit on the 61st Infantry unit, rotating it so that it shows three steps of strength.

**D—** In Juhader [28] the Attacker conducts ground fire. The Syrian 112th Infantry unit is supported by an Air Support asset. The Syrian player also has an engineering asset, but he decides to not fire with that asset since he wishes to use it to bridge the Anti-Tank Ditch in its area instead of firing.

- **112th Infantry** has +1 (for being supported by ground support mission), –1 (for firing unit conducting a crossing assault) and –1 (target’s point unit is an infantry-supported strongpoint) for a net SN of 5+1–1–1=4 and fires with 4 dice. This is “4 tries for a 4” — rolls 3, 4, 6, 9 — two hits!

The Israeli player has two hits to resolve. Since the strongpoint has only two steps, it must be eliminated (no retreat option). The strongpoint is placed in the Eliminated Box. The Syrian player has now opened a gap in the Israeli line of defence and may take advantage of the situation either during the Breakthrough Movement Phase or the next Game Turn.

**E—** Since the 5th Engineering asset survived both artillery and ground fire during the combat, it may now bridge the AT ditch by placing an AT Ditch Bridged marker on one of the two AT ditch boundaries adjoining the area of combat. He chooses the AT ditch boundary with the road connection (this is allowed even though the attack into the area did not come across this boundary), which now is considered as an open boundary with a road connection.

**F—** In Tel Saki [27] the Attacker now conducts ground fire. The Syrian force will benefit from the disordered strongpoint.

- **61st Infantry** has –1 (firing unit conducting a crossing assault), +1 (target’s point unit is an armor-supported strongpoint) and –1 (veteran firing on elite) for a net SN of 5–1+1–1=4 and fires with 3 dice. This is “3 tries for a 4” — rolls 2, 5, 6 — one hit!
- **5th Tank** has +1 (firer is armor), –1 (firing unit conducting a crossing assault), +1 (target’s point unit is an armor-supported strongpoint) and –2 (green firing on elite) for a net SN of 5+1–1+1–2=4. This is “1 try for a 4” — rolls 7 — miss!

The Israeli player has one hit to resolve, which reduces the strongpoint by one step. The GHQ Engineering asset bridges the AT ditch (since it didn’t fire during the ground fire step).

### Example 11 — Electronic Warfare Event

**Overview—** The Electronic Warfare (EW) event is a powerful event in the game available to both players. This event may be played as part of the one allowable event in combat for each player. We take a look at the event in the following example, starting with the EW event played by the Defender.

**A—** The Syrian player (Attacker) must assign assets and/or one event to the battle in Sindiana [37] first and assigns one battle asset (7th AT) and the one allowable event to the battle by placing the EW event counter in the area.

**B—** The Israeli player (Defender) must immediately roll a die due to the Syrian EW event, but before he makes the roll, he plays an EW event of his own, as a _countermeasure_ (adding +2 to his die roll). Despite improving the odds of success, he rolls 2 (+2) = 4, which causes the Israeli Golani Infantry unit to become disordered. Since the Syrian EW event was successful and all Israeli units (in this case only one) was disordered, no artillery, air support, SAM or battle assets may be assigned by the Israeli player.

After combat has been resolved in Sindiana [37], the Syrian player conducts combat in Mt. Yosifon [41].

### Example 13 – Commando Raid

**Overview—** The Syrian player has five Commando assets available to him during the game. These can be used as regular battle assets in combat or they may conduct a commando raid in the Admin Phase. Used correctly as a commando raid, they can delay Israeli reinforcements and slow movement of Israeli units by harassing them by proper placement. The Syrian player has already placed a commando asset in Ma’le Gamla [24] in the previous Admin Phase of his Player Turn. For this example, the combat round of the Commando Raid will not be illustrated.

**A—** In the Admin Phase the Syrian player places a commando asset from his Available Box in El-Al [21] to conduct a raid there in order to slow Israeli movement entering the southern Golan Heights. This is possible since the area is adjacent to a Syrian unit and it is vacant.

**B—** In the Israeli Operational Movement Phase, the 4th Mech unit moves into Ma’le Gamla [24] up across the escarpment by paying 3 MP (1 MP for road connection, +1 MP for crossing an escarpment boundary and +1 MP for the commando asset). This move by the Israeli unit causes the Syrian player to make a die roll for the raid, rolls 2 — Raid successful! The raid is resolved during the ensuing Combat Phase, in which the commando asset will fight as a defended roadblock.

**Tip:** Mark a successful raid by placing the Israeli unit face up and mark a partly successful raid by leaving the unit upright.

**C—** During the Strategic Movement Phase, the Israeli player moves the 9th Mech unit marked as a reserve in the Southern Assembly Area along road connections upward and along the Golan escarpment. The mech unit may not strat-move into El-Al [21] as units are not allowed to either strat-move or retreat into areas occupied by commando assets conducting raids. Thus, the unit must stop its movement in Givat Yoav [19].

In the Combat Phase the raid is resolved.

### Example 14 — Reaction & Breakthrough

**Overview—** After all combats have been resolved, the Non-phasing player has the opportunity to move units marked with a reserve marker in reaction to what the Phasing player has accomplished so far. He may not move his units into an uncontested enemy area and cause combat. The opportunity for the Phasing player to move units in the Breakthrough Movement Phase is limited to the following conditions: the player may move units (i) marked with an exploitation marker, (ii) marked with a reserve marker or (iii) by expending a Special Action. If this movement causes an area to be contested, the combat in this area is mandatory. A round of Breakthrough Combat (which is otherwise just like regular Combat) must occur due to Breakthrough Movement and it may occur if the Phasing player plays a Special Action in an already contested area. For the examples below, none of the combat rounds of the Breakthrough Combat Phase will be illustrated.

**A—** After the normal Combat Phase is complete, the Breakthrough Phases are conducted. The Non-phasing player has the opportunity to react (with units marked with a Reserve marker) to the potential points of breakthrough prior to the Breakthrough Movement Phase. The Syrian player had previously marked the 91st Tank unit in Tel Faris [30] with the Syrian Army Reserve marker (during a Syrian Operational Movement Phase). Given the developing situation, the Syrian player decides to reinforce Einot Peham [31] with the 91st Tank unit in Tel Faris [30], trying to prevent the destruction of a large unit (the loss of that single-strength infantry unit will yield an Israeli VP). A reacting unit (marked in reserve) may make a full operational move during the Reaction Phase, including entering into a contested area, but may not create a newly contested area.

**B—** Now the Israeli player begins the Breakthrough Movement Phase. There are two ways to move during this phase: (i) units marked in reserve may make a full operational move and (ii) armor units marked with an exploitation marker or subject to the play of a Special Action may move one area (as exploitation units). In both cases, the Phasing player may create newly contested areas. Given the Syrian choice to reinforce Einot Peham [31] during the Reaction Phase, the Israeli player decides to move a unit into that area to keep pressure on the point of attack. He moves the 317th Para unit marked in reserve in Yonatan [26] into Einot Peham [31], with an expectation of securing the elimination of one of the Syrian units or better. The Northern Command reserve marker is removed.

**C—** The Syrian player now conducts some congestion management in order to prepare for additional attacks on the Syrian line. The Israeli Armor in Zohar [33] was marked with an exploitation marker from a successful combat (in the just preceding Combat Phase) in which the Syrian unit in the area was eliminated by at least one more step loss than necessary. The Israeli player moves the 205th Armor from Zohar [33] into Keshet [32] to create a newly contested area.

**D—** The Israeli player decides to use the Reusable Special Action (placing the block into the Used Box) to advance the 670th Mech unit in Tel Saki [27] one area forward into Juhader [28] to pressure a weak part of the Syrian position. Note that if this unit had been marked in reserve, it could have made a dramatic operational movement into Tel Faris [30], surrounding units in the Syrian line.

**E—** Finally the Israeli player decides to move the [?205th?] Armor unit marked in reserve in [?Ma’ale?] Gamla [24] forward to Yonatan [26] costing 1 MP (road connection) and then on to Einot Peham [31] costing 3 MP (2 MP for the field connection and +1 MP for entering an occupied area). The Northern Command reserve marker is removed. Note that limits on reserve units apply only to Strategic Movement — both moves with the reserve units were operational moves.

### Example 15 — Supply

**Overview—** The Supply Phase affects players during their own player turn, so it is important to take actions that leave your units supplied during your own Supply Phase. The worst effects do not immediately take hold, however, which allows players to consider bolder moves. Note that the units in this example are placed to show aspects of the three supply path tracing sub-cases in the supply rules. Also assume that the Syrian supply lines traces out of the image to Entry areas Z and Y (as valid supply sources).

**A—** The Syrian 7th Armored Recon unit in Kuneitra [49] traces directly through friendly-controlled areas to the valid supply source Entry area Y (Maaz [63]).

- Uses 14.93 – only trace across an unbridged AT ditch boundary if the tracing unit/area is adjacent to that boundary.

**B—** The Syrian 68th Infantry unit in El Rom [56] traces out of the contested area to Tel Hermonit [57] across the bridged AT ditch boundary to a valid supply source.

- Uses 9.31c — out of an occupied enemy-controlled area (including enemy units).
- Uses 14.93 — the supply path traces across a bridged AT ditch boundary.

**C—** The Moroccan (Moroc) Infantry unit in Shamir [3] does have a successful supply path, although its position is vulnerable to become isolated.

- The path to Zaura [54] is blocked by the escarpment boundary (so the restriction 14.93 comes into play).
- However, the path into Banias [2] and across the escarpment boundary is valid since a road crosses the escarpment there.
- Uses 9.31c — into, through _and_ out of an occupied friendly-controlled area (including enemy units).
- Uses 14.93 — the supply path traces across a bridged AT ditch boundary.

**D—** The Syrian 78th Tank unit in Wasit [52] must be marked as unsupplied.

- The paths into Qela [53] and Sikeik [55] are blocked since they are under enemy control (so the restriction 9.31b comes into play — the path cannot be traced into or through these areas).
- Uses 9.31c — out of an occupied enemy-controlled area (including enemy units). Then into Merom Golan [51], this is valid since the area is friendly-controlled.
- The path into Booster [50] is blocked by the minefield and the Israeli Strongpoint unit there (so restrictions 14.93 and and 9.31b comes into play).
- The path into Kuneitra [49] is valid but from there the supply path ends (not adjacent to an unbridged AT ditch, 14.93).

**E—** The 85th Infantry is also marked as unsupplied as its supply path is blocked in the same way as the 78th Armored unit above. Assume that no valid path exists through the bottom edge of the illustration.
